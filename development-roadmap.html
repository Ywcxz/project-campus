Development Roadmap

An evidence-driven path from concept to decision

The CAMPUS roadmap organizes the transition from conceptual consolidation to bounded research, technical experiments, functional prototyping, validation, institutional evaluation, and selective expansion.

It is intentionally an R&D funnel rather than a conventional feature roadmap. Later stages are earned by evidence, and the project must remain able to narrow, redirect, pause, or stop.

Roadmap Principle

Concept → Evidence → Architecture → Experiment → Prototype → Validation → Institutional Decision → Selective Expansion.

Current development position

CAMPUS is moving from conceptual exploration toward structured R&D and prototyping. The blueprint, conceptual architecture, technical direction, institutional context, bounded MVP, and research methodology establish the foundation for the next stages.

The roadmap must not assume fixed timelines, institutional access, manpower, funding, infrastructure, or adoption. Those conditions are themselves subjects for investigation.

Roadmap stages

Stage 0 — Blueprint Consolidation. Establish a coherent, evidence-disciplined R&D reference, including the problem, architecture, MVP boundary, risks, questions, and decisions.

Stage 1 — Institutional and User Discovery. Understand users, workflows, systems, authorities, constraints, stakeholders, institutional context, and relevant research. Output: an evidence-backed problem and opportunity map.

Stage 2 — Architecture and Technical Specification. Refine the entity, relationship, provenance, permissions, integration, deployment, and technical models using discovery findings.

Stage 3 — Technical Experiments. Resolve high-risk assumptions involving representation, traversal, search, provenance, authorization, temporal relationships, integration, synchronization, local-first operation, and extensibility.

Stage 4 — MVP Prototype. Build the smallest functional system capable of testing the central hypothesis through identity, relationships, search, relational navigation, context, provenance, permissions, place, and action.

Stage 5 — User and Technical Validation. Test representative tasks and measure discovery success, effort, comprehension, responsiveness, data integrity, authorization, synchronization, recovery, resource use, maintainability, and deployment complexity.

Stage 6 — Institutional Validation and Pilot Decision. Determine whether the use case addresses a real need, can coexist with existing systems, has clear authority and governance, and is appropriate for controlled institutional experimentation.

Stage 7 — Selective Expansion. Expand only validated dimensions such as Digital Space and Knowledge, CCLR, Pathways, Research, Spatial capabilities, Local-First infrastructure, GIS, Analytics, or AI. The ordering is not fixed and must respond to evidence.

Prototype vertical slice

Search → Person or Entity → Related Organization, Course, or Project → Knowledge or Facility → Context and Provenance → Opportunity or Service → Action

The prototype should implement a limited set of people, organizations, programs or courses, knowledge resources, places and facilities, activities, services, opportunities, and selected research or project objects where justified.

Data progression

  • 01 Synthetic data for architecture, interface, and relationship testing.
  • 02 Public or non-sensitive information where appropriate.
  • 03 Controlled institutional information after permission.
  • 04 Authorized pilot data under defined governance.

This progression allows technical and UX research to proceed without prematurely creating institutional data risks.

What the MVP should not attempt

The MVP should not become a university ERP, student information system, LMS, complete learning-record system, research-management platform, full alumni system, complete GIS, digital twin, production AI assistant, universal social network, replacement portal, complete offline university, or full integration of every institutional system.

Development dependencies

The approximate dependency order is problem validation, institutional and systems understanding, relational model validation, identity and provenance, permissions, search and discovery, the MVP vertical slice, user and technical validation, an institutional pilot decision, and selected dimension expansion.

Research, architecture, product design, engineering, governance, and institutional engagement can proceed in parallel, but decision gates should not be bypassed.

Planning and environments

Because access and resources are unresolved, planning should use relative stages rather than unsupported fixed commitments. Milestones should state their objective, inputs, activities, deliverables, evidence, risks, decision criteria, decision, and next action.

The project should progress from local development, to a controlled prototype, demonstration environment, research test environment, institutional pilot, and production only if adoption and operational requirements justify it.

Change, risk, and resource gates

Changes should be classified as minor, architectural, scope, hypothesis, institutional, or strategic. Development priority should consider impact, uncertainty, and dependency. Identity, provenance, relationships, authority, and integration boundaries have high dependency and should be investigated early.

Before each major stage, assess developers, research and design capacity, institutional support, infrastructure, budget, data access, and stakeholder availability. When resources are insufficient, reduce scope rather than creating unsupported commitments.

Stop and expansion conditions

Development should pause or change direction if the problem cannot be validated, an existing system already solves it, relational discovery provides no measurable benefit, technical complexity is disproportionate, privacy or security risks cannot be addressed, institutional access is unavailable, governance cannot be established, or a better-supported approach emerges.

Expansion beyond the MVP should require user evidence, technical evidence, institutional evidence, and governance evidence. If one is substantially missing, expansion should remain conditional.

Roadmap Decision Logic

Is there a meaningful problem? Is the relational approach useful? Can it be implemented? Can it coexist responsibly? Does the prototype demonstrate value? Is institutional experimentation justified? Each answer determines whether CAMPUS expands, revises, narrows, remains research-only, or stops.

Current roadmap status

Established directions include a bounded MVP, stage-gated development, evidence-driven decisions, progressive data access, technical experiments before major commitments, comparative validation, and institutional evaluation before a pilot.

Exact schedules, team size, funding, infrastructure, technical stack, pilot environment, approval process, quantitative thresholds, and operational ownership remain unresolved.

Roadmap Conclusion

The immediate objective is not to build the complete digital-campus vision. It is to produce a small, technically credible, researchable prototype capable of answering whether a persistent, permission-aware, provenance-aware relational layer creates measurable value while appropriately coexisting with existing institutional systems.
