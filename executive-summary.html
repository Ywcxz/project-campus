Executive Summary

From conceptual coherence to evidence

CAMPUS is an independently initiated research and development concept for an experimental digital-campus architecture for universities. It investigates whether a persistent, relationship-oriented digital layer can improve how people discover, connect, interact with, and maintain continuity across the university's people, organizations, educational activities, knowledge, research, places, facilities, services, opportunities, and institutional resources.

Universities already contain these resources, but they are often distributed across different systems, offices, communities, physical locations, and information environments. CAMPUS investigates whether the missing capability is not another standalone system, but a persistent layer capable of representing, discovering, and navigating relationships across the university environment.

Core Approach

CAMPUS proposes an infrastructure for relationships, not another collection of isolated applications. It should work alongside specialized institutional systems while preserving authority, provenance, identity, permissions, privacy, and security.

The CAMPUS approach

The proposed architecture is relationship-oriented, extensible, interoperable, provenance-aware, permission-aware, human-centered, and research-driven. Its major assumptions should be tested through evidence, prototypes, experiments, stakeholder research, and documented validation.

At its foundation, CAMPUS proposes a relational model connecting people, organizations, education, knowledge, research, places, activities, services, opportunities, and communities. Potential dimensions include a digital community and knowledge layer, curriculum continuity and learning records, education-to-career pathways, research discovery, spatial campus capabilities, local-first infrastructure, GIS, analytics, and future AI.

These dimensions are possible areas of development, not a commitment to implement all of them. The common foundation is more important: identity, relationships, knowledge, discovery, place, search, interaction, provenance, permissions, and interoperability.

Bounded R&D and MVP

The current phase is moving from conceptual exploration toward structured R&D and prototyping. The immediate objective is not to build a complete digital university, but to develop a small functional vertical slice capable of testing the core hypothesis.

The MVP is expected to focus on identity and basic roles, people and organizations, knowledge and institutional or community information, places and campus resources, relationships among entities, search and discovery, basic community interaction, provenance and information context, permissions, and a limited mechanism for interaction with or reference to external institutional systems.

A complete digital twin, comprehensive learning records, full education-to-career pathways, advanced research collaboration, large-scale GIS analytics, and sophisticated AI are not assumed to be MVP requirements. They may be investigated later where evidence justifies them.

Existing university systems

CAMPUS does not assume that universities should replace existing specialized systems. Enrollment, finance, human resources, learning management, research repositories, library services, identity infrastructure, and other institutional systems may remain authoritative for their respective domains.

The actual relationship between CAMPUS and existing VSU systems remains unresolved until institutional discovery establishes their ownership, authoritative data sources, interfaces, identity infrastructure, governance requirements, security constraints, interoperability capabilities, planned initiatives, and priorities.

Institutional and architectural context

CAMPUS is being developed with reference to the Visayas State University Strategic Plan 2017-2027 as a strategic context and alignment framework. It does not claim that the Strategic Plan requires or mandates CAMPUS. The R&D instead investigates whether a relational architecture could support selected directions involving education, science and technology, communities, governance, innovation spaces, resources, and alumni relationships.

VSU DIGITS is treated as relevant institutional context, but its internal architecture, implementation status, ownership, interfaces, and relationship to CAMPUS must not be assumed without evidence. Possible relationships remain open for investigation.

A local-first or hybrid model is another architectural direction under investigation. Selected capabilities could operate within university-controlled infrastructure or local networks while synchronizing appropriate information with external systems when connectivity is available. The benefits and requirements of this approach must be established through research.

Research program

CAMPUS is fundamentally an R&D project rather than a predetermined software-development exercise. The research must determine whether the relational problem exists in meaningful form, which relationships matter most to users, what information should remain separated, how existing systems can participate without losing authority, what architecture is practical, and what privacy, security, provenance, and governance mechanisms are required.

The project must remain capable of narrowing, modifying, or disproving its assumptions. The prototype is both a software artifact and a research instrument. It should demonstrate a small number of coherent relationships and user journeys, producing evidence about feasibility, usability, discovery behavior, permissions, interoperability, infrastructure, and implementation effort.

Expected Contribution

A coherent digital-campus architecture, a validated or revised relational model, a bounded prototype, implementation evidence, research findings, integration evidence, governance and privacy models, local-first assessment, and a roadmap informed by validation.

Current R&D position

CAMPUS should currently be understood as a proposed research and development architecture for a relationship-oriented digital layer over the university, being investigated through a bounded prototype and institutional and technical validation.

It is not an officially adopted VSU platform, an approved university system, an established institutional architecture, a replacement for existing systems, a validated solution to every university information problem, or a complete product ready for institution-wide deployment.

CAMPUS investigates whether a persistent, permission-aware, and provenance-aware relational digital layer can connect the university's people, knowledge, education, research, places, services, opportunities, and resources in ways that improve discovery, interaction, and continuity, while interoperating with—not replacing— existing institutional systems.
