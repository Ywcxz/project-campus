Manpower, Infrastructure & Resources

The minimum capability required to generate credible evidence

This chapter defines the human, technical, infrastructure, organizational, and research resources potentially required to execute the CAMPUS R&D roadmap.

The resource model is deliberately staged. CAMPUS does not require a full institutional software organization to investigate its central hypothesis. The immediate objective is a small, capable R&D team able to conduct research, build a functional prototype, and generate credible technical and user evidence.

Resource Planning Principle

Start with the minimum capability required to generate credible evidence. Increase staffing, infrastructure, and operational requirements only when validated scope justifies them.

Resource levels

The resource model has three broad levels: R&D and prototype, institutional pilot, and production or scale. Only the first level is immediately necessary. Later levels should be introduced when scope, integration, security, deployment scale, operations, funding, and institutional responsibility require them.

Core capability areas

  • 01 Research: problem discovery, user research, institutional research, and validation.
  • 02 Product and architecture: problem definition, relational modeling, and system boundaries.
  • 03 Software engineering: frontend, backend, database, APIs, search, and integration.
  • 04 Infrastructure: deployment, networking, monitoring, backup, and security.
  • 05 Design: interaction, visualization, usability, and accessibility.
  • 06 Governance: privacy, permissions, provenance, data governance, and security.
  • 07 Institutional coordination: stakeholders, systems, permissions, and pilot coordination.

Minimum R&D team

A credible early prototype can potentially be developed by a small multidisciplinary team consisting of an R&D or product lead, a full-stack engineer, a systems or architecture engineer, a UX or product designer, a research or validation lead, a security and governance adviser, and an institutional or technical liaison.

Several roles may be combined during early experimentation, such as R&D and product, architecture and backend, research and UX, or infrastructure and security. This is appropriate initially but may become unsustainable as scope increases.

Role responsibilities

The R&D or product lead maintains the relationship between problem, research, architecture, prototype, evidence, and decision. Engineering covers responsive interfaces, entity and relationship services, permissions, provenance, APIs, data, indexing, migrations, integrity, external references, imports, and future synchronization.

Architecture covers conceptual-to-technical translation, data, integration, identity, security, deployment, and extensibility. UX and research investigate search, relationship comprehension, contextual navigation, cognitive load, provenance, spatial interaction, actionability, accessibility, interviews, observation, experiments, and evidence synthesis.

Institutional coordination establishes appropriate contacts, system owners, data authorities, ICT stakeholders, approval pathways, and pilot requirements without claiming authority on behalf of VSU. Security and governance address authentication, authorization, classification, privacy, provenance, auditability, threat modeling, secure development, retention, correction, and deletion.

Specialized future capabilities

GIS, spatial computing, data science, AI or machine learning, cybersecurity, distributed systems, DevOps or SRE, learning technology, and research-information specialists are not necessarily required for the MVP. They should be introduced when a validated use case requires them.

AI as a development multiplier

AI and LLM tools may accelerate coding, documentation, architecture exploration, testing, research synthesis, prototyping, data modeling, debugging, interface iteration, and project documentation.

They do not replace problem definition, institutional access, stakeholder relationships, permission, accountability, validation, human research judgment, technical review, or governance decisions. The appropriate model is human direction, AI-assisted execution, and evidence-based review.

Scaling human resources

Core R&D may involve one product or R&D lead, one or two engineers, one UX or research contributor, and part-time architecture, security, governance, or institutional support. An institutional pilot may add dedicated frontend, backend, integration, infrastructure, security, governance, coordination, and data expertise. Production would require broader engineering, operations, support, UX, QA, documentation, training, and service-management capacity.

MVP infrastructure

Modest infrastructure may include developer workstations, source control, issue tracking, development and test environments, application and database services, search or indexing, storage, networking, HTTPS, logs, monitoring, backups, secrets management, access controls, encrypted connections, and secure development practices.

The initial system should run in a controlled local or development environment to support rapid experimentation, reproducibility, synthetic data, demonstrations, and portability. Containerized development may help reproduce the prototype across environments.

Hosting and data resources

Potential hosting environments include developer-hosted systems, controlled cloud infrastructure, university-hosted environments, and local-first test environments. None should be treated as the permanent production architecture at this stage.

Data should progress from synthetic and public information to controlled institutional information and authorized pilot data only when appropriate permission, governance, and security controls exist. The guiding rule is minimum necessary data, controlled access, clear authority, and documented provenance.

Testing and documentation

Testing should cover functional behavior, data and relationship correctness, search, authorization, security, UX, integration, resilience, and performance. Local-first experiments may require local nodes, synchronization, conflict resolution, identity synchronization, encryption, recovery, registration, and monitoring.

The project should maintain the R&D Blueprint, architecture and API documentation, data model, research log, decision and risk registers, test results, user research findings, deployment documentation, governance documentation, and prototype changelog alongside development.

Resource selection and phasing

Major resource decisions should consider research value, technical suitability, cost, maintainability, portability, security, privacy, institutional compatibility, developer capability, and replaceability. Requirements should grow with validated scope: discovery first, technical experiments next, MVP engineering and UX afterward, institutional validation later, and dedicated operations only for a justified pilot or production system.

Constraints and scope control

Likely constraints include manpower, funding, institutional access, APIs, test environments, restricted data, networks, security, maintenance, and stakeholder availability. The correct response is to reduce scope before reducing research quality.

  • Tier 1 Entity and relationship model, identity, permissions, provenance, search, navigation, place, and validation.
  • Tier 2 Richer community interaction, events, opportunities, service routing, and spatial interface.
  • Tier 3 CCLR, Pathways, Research, local-first expansion, and advanced GIS.
  • Tier 4 Digital twin, advanced analytics, AI, and large-scale automation.

Resource readiness

Before entering the MVP stage, assess R&D leadership, engineering, UX and research, architecture, security, institutional liaison, development environment, hosting, test data, participants, institutional access, funding, and pilot infrastructure. Several of these are currently to be established or unresolved rather than assumed ready.

Resource Status

CAMPUS requires multidisciplinary capability, but the MVP does not require a full production university organization. AI can accelerate work, while human research, governance, institutional, and technical responsibility remains essential.

Minimum viable R&D organization

R&D / Product Lead → Research, Architecture, Engineering, UX / HCI, Data / Security → Institutional Liaison and Advisers

This is a capability model rather than a required hierarchy. One person may initially occupy several roles. If the project reaches an institutional pilot, responsibilities should become more explicit and include institutional, ICT, data-governance, security, and participating-unit representation.

Resource Conclusion

The immediate requirement is a small but multidisciplinary R&D capability able to investigate the problem, understand the institutional environment, test the relational architecture, build a functional prototype, validate it, document evidence, and decide what deserves continuation. Start small, generate evidence, and increase resources only when justified.
