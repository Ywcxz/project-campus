Research Questions

What the CAMPUS R&D program must learn

The research questions define what the CAMPUS R&D program is attempting to learn. They are not product requirements and should not be interpreted as evidence that the proposed architecture is already validated.

They guide institutional discovery, user research, architectural design, technical experiments, prototype development, usability evaluation, governance analysis, integration investigation, and decisions about whether and how CAMPUS should proceed beyond the experimental stage.

Primary Research Question · Proposed

Can a persistent, permission-aware, and provenance-aware relational digital layer improve the discovery, understanding, interaction, and continuity of relationships among people, knowledge, education, research, places, services, opportunities, and communities within a university while interoperating appropriately with existing institutional systems?

Problem and user need

What information, relationships, and university resources do students, faculty, staff, researchers, administrators, alumni, and other stakeholders currently have difficulty discovering or navigating? How do they discover people, knowledge, services, opportunities, facilities, organizations, research activities, and other resources today?

Where does friction appear when information or relationships cross organizational, technological, or physical boundaries? Which relationships do users actually consider useful, and which groups experience the strongest need for cross-system discovery?

The relational model

What entities and relationships are necessary to represent valuable forms of university discovery and interaction? Which relationships should persist, and which should remain temporary or contextual?

Candidate relationships include membership, authorship, teaching, enrollment, collaboration, participation, location, ownership, responsibility, prerequisite, association, recommendation, availability, temporal connection, and institutional authority. These are research categories, not a finalized vocabulary.

How should changes over time be represented without destroying useful historical context or creating unnecessary retention? Can one common relational model support multiple CAMPUS dimensions without becoming unnecessarily complex?

Discovery and user experience

Does relationship-oriented navigation help users discover relevant information, people, resources, or opportunities that they would not otherwise find easily? Does relational context improve understanding, and does it reduce the effort required to move from discovery to a useful action?

Which interaction patterns are effective: search, linked entity navigation, contextual recommendations, maps, timelines, graphs, organizational views, profiles, activity feeds, or structured pathways? When does exposing more relationships improve discovery, and when does it create information overload?

Continuity

Can a relational architecture preserve useful continuity across changes in people, courses, programs, projects, organizations, facilities, and activities? Which relationships remain valuable after an immediate activity or institutional transaction has ended?

What forms of continuity matter most: curriculum and learning history, research participation, organizational participation, mentorship, alumni relationships, education-to-career pathways, institutional knowledge, or community relationships? What should expire, become inaccessible, or lose visibility as relationships change?

Knowledge and provenance

How should CAMPUS distinguish official institutional information, community-generated knowledge, personal opinions, unverified information, and scholarly knowledge? What provenance is necessary for users to judge authority, context, and reliability?

Who should be authorized to create, modify, verify, endorse, or retire different categories of information? How should conflicting or outdated information be represented and resolved? Can a more connected environment improve discovery without increasing misinformation, misattribution, or unauthorized disclosure?

Institutional systems and interoperability

What existing university systems should CAMPUS connect to, reference, index, synchronize with, or remain independent from? Which systems should remain authoritative for particular information, and what integration mechanisms are technically and institutionally feasible?

Candidate approaches include APIs, federated identity, metadata exchange, event-based synchronization, controlled replication, indexing, and links to authoritative systems. The research must also determine how CAMPUS can avoid becoming an unnecessary duplicate system of record.

Local-first and hybrid architecture

Under what conditions would local-first or hybrid operation provide meaningful benefits? What are the requirements for resilience, connectivity, infrastructure control, synchronization, conflict resolution, security, maintenance, and governance?

Which capabilities genuinely need local operation, and which can depend on external services when connectivity is available? This is an architectural investigation, not an established deployment decision.

Security, privacy, and governance

What privacy, security, identity, authorization, consent, retention, and data-stewardship mechanisms are required? Who can create, validate, access, modify, connect, or remove information and relationships?

How should the project handle conflicting authorities, disputes, moderation, institutional accountability, user control, and the right to limit visibility? What governance arrangements are appropriate for a system that crosses institutional and community boundaries?

Technical feasibility and value

Can the proposed model represent and query useful relationships at acceptable levels of performance, reliability, security, and maintainability? What architecture, data model, interfaces, and infrastructure are required?

Does the resulting experience produce measurable improvements in discovery, understanding, interaction, continuity, or access to resources that justify its technical and institutional complexity? Which outcomes would count as evidence of value, and what findings would cause the project to narrow, modify, or reject its assumptions?

Initial R&D Priority

Begin with institutional discovery, representative user workflows, a small set of high-value relationships, and a bounded prototype. The research should establish what problem exists before expanding the architecture around what might be possible.

Scope of This Chapter

The questions establish the research agenda for Blueprint v0.1. Subsequent chapters should map each priority question to evidence, methods, prototypes, decision points, and criteria for disconfirmation.
