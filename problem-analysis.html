Chapter 04 · Awaiting Data

Problem Analysis

Where the Evidence Belongs, Once It Exists

This chapter is deliberately incomplete, and stays that way on purpose. Its job in the document's argument is to convert the general claims made in Chapter Three — that the same twenty or thirty questions get asked by every incoming batch, that the informal layer absorbs problems the administrative layer never touches — into specific, counted, sourced evidence. That evidence does not exist yet in a form rigorous enough to publish, and Chapter Six already commits this document to treating institutional and anecdotal claims as things to verify before they are stated as fact. Writing this chapter early, without that evidence, would mean breaking that commitment in the one chapter most responsible for upholding it.

What follows is not a summary of findings. It is a specification of what this chapter needs before it can become one.

What This Chapter Will Contain

When written in full, this chapter will present a categorized analysis of a defined sample of posts drawn from the Facebook groups and pages incoming and current students actually use — counted by category (housing, enrollment, scholarships, organizations, lost items, and others as they emerge), and specifically tracking how often a thread's most useful reply is simply a redirect to an office rather than an answer in itself. It will present findings from a defined number of structured interviews with students, faculty, and staff across specific offices — Registrar, USSO, the Housing Office among them — asked consistent questions about where information currently breaks down. It will present results from a survey instrument, run across a stated number of respondents, built to test whether the problems this document assumes are actually the ones students experience as most burdensome.

What Will Not Appear Here in the Meantime

No percentage, frequency, or count will be published in this chapter until it is backed by a method a reader could, in principle, repeat and check. This document has already used one real example of what happens when institutional data is published without that discipline — the beds-and-rooms mislabeling described in Chapter Six — and has no intention of repeating that mistake in the chapter whose entire purpose is to establish the problem CAMPUS is solving.

A placeholder chapter is a visible gap. An invented one would be an invisible error, discovered later and at greater cost. Between the two, the gap is the honest choice, and it is the one this document makes.
