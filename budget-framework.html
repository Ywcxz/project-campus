Budget Framework

Funding the evidence before funding the whole vision

This chapter establishes the financial framework for CAMPUS development. It does not present a final project cost because the actual budget depends on validated scope, manpower, duration, infrastructure, institutional requirements, data access, security, pilot scale, and funding arrangements.

The budget should evolve with the evidence and development roadmap. It distinguishes R&D and prototype costs, institutional pilot costs, production and operational costs, and future expansion costs.

Budgeting Principle

Do not budget the complete vision before validating the foundational hypothesis. First establish the cost of generating credible evidence.

Budget structure

The main cost categories are personnel; research and validation; software and development tools; infrastructure; security, privacy, and governance; institutional integration; training and documentation; operations and maintenance; and contingency.

Cost categories

Personnel is likely to be the largest R&D cost and may include the R&D or product lead, engineers, architecture, UX, research, QA, infrastructure, security, institutional liaison, and specialist advisers. Costs depend on number of people, duration, compensation, consultant rates, institutional contributions, and participation models.

Research and validation may include recruitment, transportation, communication, materials, surveys, interviews, usability testing, workshops, documentation, data preparation, assistance, and dissemination. Institutional facilities or personnel provided without additional cash cost should still be recorded as in-kind resources.

Software and tooling may include source control, project management, design, development and testing tools, databases, search, monitoring, security, domains, certificates, AI services, and commercial APIs. Open-source tools can reduce licensing costs but still carry hosting, maintenance, support, integration, security, and developer costs.

Infrastructure can include development machines, servers, cloud or virtual machines, local-first nodes, database and object storage, backups, networking, monitoring, logging, recovery, and deployment. A local prototype and a university-wide production system must not share the same infrastructure assumption.

Security and privacy need explicit budget lines for threat modeling, assessment, testing, scanning, review, identity, encryption, audit logging, secure hosting, and compliance-related work. Institutional integration may add system analysis, mapping, APIs, identity, synchronization, migration, testing, security review, coordination, and change management.

Training, documentation, support, hosting, storage, monitoring, maintenance, subscriptions, data stewardship, and administration must be separated into one-time and recurring costs. Contingency should reflect the maturity and uncertainty of the project rather than being fixed prematurely.

Cost by development stage

  • 01 Blueprint and discovery: research, coordination, and documentation.
  • 02 Technical experiments: engineering and development infrastructure.
  • 03 MVP: personnel, software, infrastructure, UX, and testing.
  • 04 User validation: research, participants, and evaluation.
  • 05 Institutional validation: integration, security, governance, and coordination.
  • 06 Pilot: integration, infrastructure, support, training, and monitoring.
  • 07 Production: operations, security, maintenance, support, and infrastructure.
  • 08 Expansion: specialized engineering and domain capabilities.

Budget scenarios

Lean Independent R&D is the smallest credible path: a small team, synthetic or public data, existing hardware, low-cost tooling, modest hosting, limited consulting, and no production institutional integration. Its purpose is to generate evidence about the core hypothesis.

Supported Prototype assumes additional institutional, research, grant, or partner support for personnel, infrastructure, UX, security, datasets, advisers, and controlled demonstrations.

Institutional Pilot adds identity and system integration, security assessment, governance, infrastructure, support, training, monitoring, administration, change management, and evaluation. It should be estimated only after a use case and participating units are defined.

Production Deployment would require dedicated engineering and operations, security operations, support, governance, monitoring, disaster recovery, integration maintenance, training, and long-term development. It is a future model, not the current budget.

Estimation and confidence

Detailed costs should use quantity multiplied by unit cost multiplied by duration. This can apply to personnel, hosting, hardware, research activities, and integration. Each estimate should state its confidence level: conceptual, preliminary, or detailed.

The current CAMPUS budget should remain conceptual or preliminary until scope, manpower, infrastructure, staffing arrangements, quotations, and institutional requirements are known.

Assumptions and uncertainty

Significant assumptions should be recorded rather than silently converted into budget facts. Examples include a small initial team, available development hardware, sufficient open-source tools, adequate synthetic or public data, available APIs, university pilot infrastructure, security assessment requirements, hosting, and local-first hardware.

The largest uncertainties are likely to be personnel duration, institutional integration, security, infrastructure, identity integration, data preparation, institutional support, pilot scale, local-first complexity, and long-term operations.

Budget gates

Before the MVP, budget primarily for research, architecture, engineering, UX, experiments, and basic infrastructure. Before an institutional pilot, re-estimate integration, infrastructure, security, governance, support, and training. Before production, conduct a separate total-cost-of-ownership, operating-cost, staffing, and sustainability assessment.

Cost Discipline

A prototype budget must never automatically become a production budget. Each development stage must earn its next level of spending through evidence.

Total cost and sustainability

A production evaluation must consider initial development, infrastructure, integration, security, operations, maintenance, support, data governance, and future development over a defined planning period.

Financial sustainability also asks who pays for infrastructure, employs developers, maintains integrations, manages data, handles support, funds security, owns the software, and carries recurring costs if project funding ends. Recurring expense must remain proportionate to demonstrated value.

Funding and proposal readiness

Possible funding categories include independent R&D, university research, government programs, innovation grants, technology development, institutional transformation, research partnerships, external collaborators, and future commercialization where appropriate.

Before a formal proposal, CAMPUS should have a defined problem, research questions, methodology, bounded scope, technical architecture, work plan, manpower requirements, measurable outputs, budget assumptions, risks, governance, intellectual-property position, and accurately stated institutional relationship.

Financial decision framework

For every major expenditure, ask what problem it addresses, whether it is required now, what evidence supports it, whether a lower-cost alternative exists, whether it creates lock-in, whether it is reusable, what happens if the hypothesis is disproved, whether it creates recurring costs, who maintains it, and whether it can wait for a later decision gate.

Budget changes should be tracked with scope changes. The project should not increase spending merely to preserve features that evidence no longer supports.

Current budget status

Established positions include that no final production budget is yet justified, resources must be staged, personnel will likely be a major R&D cost, integration and security can materially change later costs, and one-time and recurring costs must be distinguished.

Proposed positions include four budget scenarios, explicit cost categories, progressive refinement at gates, assumption and confidence tracking, and total-cost-of-ownership analysis before production. Actual personnel costs, funding, duration, hosting, integration, security, pilot scale, and long-term operations remain unresolved.

MVP-to-pilot cost boundary

The MVP is a research environment. It begins when resources are committed to the functional vertical slice and ends when enough evidence exists to decide whether institutional experimentation is justified.

MVP costs may cover prototype software, synthetic or public data, prototype authentication and permissions, the relational model, search, navigation, provenance, basic places, limited activities or opportunities, development infrastructure, controlled demonstrations, usability testing, and technical experiments.

The MVP does not require production university identity, unrestricted institutional data, production integrations, institution-wide deployment, guaranteed production availability, institutional support obligations, enterprise security certification, complete disaster recovery, university-wide training, or production service management.

The pilot begins when CAMPUS is authorized to operate with real institutional users, systems, data, infrastructure, or workflows under a defined institutional arrangement. It may require an approved use case, participating unit, data authority, identity integration, controlled infrastructure, security and privacy review, governance, support, monitoring, recovery, training, duration, and success and failure criteria.

Boundary Test

If CAMPUS can be safely demonstrated with synthetic, public, or project-created data without relying on institutional production systems, it remains within the MVP/R&D boundary. Real identity, personal data, production integration, operational infrastructure, institutional obligations, or formal user deployment move it into pilot territory.

Cost boundary by environment

  • MVP Core prototype, non-sensitive data, prototype identity, controlled hosting, baseline security, mock or test interfaces, basic monitoring and backup, research support.
  • Pilot Hardened prototype, authorized institutional data, institutional identity, controlled infrastructure, formal review, real integrations, operational support, recovery, training, and governance.
  • Production Continuous engineering, operational identity, production infrastructure, maintained integrations, full observability, service support, institutional training, and long-term governance.

What should not be charged to the MVP

Unless a specific experiment requires them, university-wide identity, ERP or LMS integration, production data migration, complete digital twin infrastructure, campus-wide hardware, institution-wide training, 24/7 support, full disaster recovery, extensive enterprise security certification, broad AI infrastructure, and complete CCLR, Pathways, or research-management platforms should remain outside the MVP budget.

Pilot trigger conditions

A pilot should be considered only after a meaningful problem, bounded use case, and prototype evidence of potential value are established; the architecture, security controls, integration, and infrastructure requirements are sufficiently understood; an institutional owner, data authority, permission path, and pilot responsibilities are identified; privacy, access, provenance, correction, and dispute processes are defined; and pilot costs, recurring costs, and funding responsibility can be estimated.

A functioning prototype alone should not trigger a pilot. Once MVP validation is complete, the prototype budget should be discarded or substantially revised rather than simply extended. The pilot needs its own estimate based on validated scope, actual integration, security and privacy requirements, infrastructure, staffing, duration, and contingency.

Future budget-planning template

Future estimates should separate MVP/R&D, pilot, and production columns for product leadership, engineering, UX and research, architecture, security and privacy, institutional coordination, tools, hosting, storage, networking, search, hardware, integration, identity, testing, training, operations, monitoring, governance, and contingency. Blank values are intentional until the relevant phase has enough information for a defensible estimate.

Every populated estimate should record its date, currency, duration, staffing and rate assumptions, infrastructure and data assumptions, institutional and in-kind contributions, one-time versus recurring costs, contingency basis, quotation or source, and confidence level.

In-kind contributions

Developer and research time, ICT support, servers, workspace, data access, testing participants, technical advice, and donated software or services should be recorded separately from cash expenditure. This distinguishes the cash requirement from the total resource requirement without making non-cash support invisible.

Financial Decision Rule

Prototype budget → evidence → pilot-specific budget → pilot evidence → production business and resource case. Approval of one budget never automatically approves the next stage.

Budget Conclusion

MVP is investment in generating evidence. Pilot is investment in testing the validated concept under controlled institutional conditions. Production is investment in sustained institutional operation. These are three different financial decisions, and a low-cost prototype budget must never imply inexpensive deployment.
