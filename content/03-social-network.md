# The Social/Academic Network

*A campus community space that VSU designs and governs*

Much of VSU's campus life already runs on a social network: questions about enrollment and scholarships, notices of suspended classes, organizations recruiting members, students selling books and looking for rooms. That network is mostly Facebook and Messenger, built for attention and conversation and governed from outside the university. [Unresolved] The centerpiece proposal of CAMPUS is VSU's own alternative for campus life: a community space where students, faculty, staff, and offices can do what they now do on Facebook, in a form designed and governed for the university, to connect relevant information to the right people and close the gap between students and the institution. [Established] (D-035) It leads the first pitch, to OVPSAS. Nothing about it has been approved, built, or tested. [Established]

> **In short.** Facebook is built to hold attention, and group chats to carry a conversation. Neither gets a question to the person who knows the answer, keeps that answer for the next batch, or shows who stands behind it and whether it is still true. The network is built for those three jobs: routing, memory, and trust. [Proposed] VSU already holds many of the pieces, from a university account for every student to a learning system, a helpdesk, and an advisory system; what it lacks is a community space that joins them, governed by VSU and its students together. [Proposed] The software is the easy part. The hard parts are people: offices that answer, helpers who stay, moderators who act, and an owner inside VSU. [Proposed] Because every answer kept makes the next question easier, the network's value compounds over years, so a pilot has to seed its memory and measure reuse, and only such a pilot can show whether students would move. [Proposed]

## The idea

### A network that already exists

The case for the network runs in five steps, each a hypothesis to test. [Proposed]

1. VSU's community life already happens on a social network, mostly Facebook and Messenger, which almost every Filipino internet user uses: 94.9% of those aged 16 and over had used Facebook in the past month, according to the Digital 2026 report ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md)). [Established]
2. That network was built for other purposes. Advertising brought in about 97.6% of Meta's revenue in 2025, its feeds are ranked to hold attention, and its group chats are built for conversation in the moment. [Established] It knows nothing of VSU's roles, offices, or rules.
3. Many of campus life's problems are problems of routing and memory: a question that should reach the person who can answer it, a notice that should reach the students it concerns and stay checkable, an answer that should carry the authority of whoever gave it and still be there next year. [Proposed]
4. A network shaped like the university, with verified members, visible roles, communities for real VSU units, labels on every post, and a memory that knows its age, could do those jobs better than a general platform. [Proposed]
5. People will move only for jobs it does clearly better, so the network should aim to win a few specific jobs, judged by the knowledge it moves rather than the attention it holds. [Proposed] (D-061)

Steps 1 and 3 need VSU evidence from the survey, the interviews, and the Facebook study ([Chapter 8.1](08a-facebook-study.md)). [Unresolved] Step 5 is what keeps the network from becoming a smaller copy of Facebook. [Proposed]

### What "social/academic" means

The network is social because it is made of people and groups talking, asking, and trading. It is academic because its shape comes from the university: faculties and departments, offices and their services, organizations and councils, dormitories and places, subjects, and the roles of student, faculty member, and staff. [Proposed] It is not a learning system, which VSU already has in VSUEE, and not a portal for official transactions, which DIGITS plans. [Established] It sits between them, as the place where campus life talks and where the university can answer. [Proposed]

Each kind of system is built to make something grow. Facebook grows attention, a group chat carries the conversation of the moment, VSUEE delivers courses, and the helpdesk resolves tickets. The network would be built to move knowledge between people and keep it where the next person can find it. [Proposed]

The closest idea in the research on shared resources is a commons: a resource a community uses under rules that the community helps make and enforce. Elinor Ostrom's study of commons that lasted for generations found they shared a handful of design principles, from clear boundaries to rules members can change and sanctions that rise step by step. [Established] (Ostrom, 1990) A campus network is that kind of resource, and [Chapter 3.2](03b-network-design.md) uses the principles to design its governance. [Proposed]

### Three jobs, and the memory that keeps them

The vision covers any purpose students, faculty, and staff have, but a first version cannot win every use case at once, and the principles allow no feature without a named reason. The author's observations point to three jobs where the network has to beat Facebook first. [Proposed] (Q-02)

1. **Reaching the people who know.** A question reaches the office that can act on it, or a person who knows the answer, whether a senior, a faculty member, or later an alumnus, without several visits and without the asker knowing in advance who that is. [Proposed] (D-071)
2. **Answers people can trust.** Office replies are marked official, other answers carry a name and a role, and an office can confirm the right one, so a wrong answer has an owner. [Proposed] (D-063)
3. **Talking across roles.** Students, faculty, and staff reach each other in one place, under rules suited to each role. [Proposed]

What makes the three worth more than a group chat is memory. In a chat, a good answer is buried within days and the next batch asks again; on the network, each answer, guide, and notice stays findable, dated, and marked when it goes out of date, so each batch starts where the last one ended. [Proposed] (D-070) Each job is a hypothesis for field research before it becomes a feature. [Chapter 3.1](03a-use-cases.md) widens the list to 29 candidate use cases. [Established]

## Why VSU needs it

These are the reasons the author expects field research to confirm, each with the first public signals behind it. None is VSU field evidence yet, and each must clear the D-038 bars before it appears on a pitch slide. [Established] (D-036, D-038)

### The student–institution gap

- **Students who cannot ask at home.** Of the 2,604 first-year students who enrolled at the Main Campus in 2026, 1,248, or 47.9%, are the first in their families to go to college, and 582 were admitted through the affirmative action pathway. [Established] Research elsewhere finds that first-generation students understand the unwritten expectations of college less well than students whose parents went, because they cannot learn them at home. [Established] (Collier and Morgan, 2008) When a parent cannot explain enrollment, scholarships, or which office does what, the answer has to come from peers or from the university, and the network is meant to make both reachable. [Proposed]
- **Offices that are hard to find.** VSU publishes a Citizen's Charter of its services and runs a helpdesk and a document request tracker. [Established] What students lack, by the author's account, is a way to learn which office handles a question before the first visit, and a place where common questions get public answers. [Unresolved] The Third SOUA says student support should also make the campus a place where students feel comfortable asking for help. [Established]
- **People who know, and are hard to find.** VSU lists its graduate faculty by department, with each one's specialization and research interest, and in January 2025 it created new research centers, among them a Geospatial and GIS Research and Services Center. [Established] A student who wants to learn GIS, or to find a thesis adviser, still has to know where to look. Students often know where an office is; what they may not know is who in the university knows about something. That is a routing problem, and the network could help with it, starting with members who choose to be found for a topic. [Proposed] (D-071)
- **The first weeks.** Student Google accounts are issued after the late enrollment period. [Established] The weeks when new students have the most questions are the weeks before they have a VSU account, a gap any design built on those accounts must close. [Proposed] (Q-38)

### Information that loses its authority

- **Many channels and no single trail.** VSU's guidelines announce typhoon suspensions through radio, television, SMS, the website, and other social media, no later than 4:30 a.m. [Established] (BOR Resolution No. 162, s. 2024) WAIS adds localized advisories for VSU account holders, with a mobile app and push notifications planned. [Established] Each channel is reasonable on its own. Together they mean a notice may reach many students as a share or a screenshot, with nothing to show whether it is still current. [Proposed] H1 of the Facebook study tests whether notices change as they travel. [Established] (D-042)
- **The website carries little of daily life.** In early October 2026, the website's announcements feed listed 21 items from July 2021 to September 2026, seven of them from 2026, mostly enrollment and exam schedules. [Established] The day-to-day notices students act on, such as office advisories, scholarship calls, and organization announcements, appear to travel elsewhere, mostly through Facebook. [Unresolved]
- **Knowledge that goes out of date.** In January 2025, VSU replaced its eight colleges with faculties. [Established] Every guide, post, and screenshot that named a college went out of date at once, with nothing to tell a reader so, and a change of rule does the same to every answer given under the old one. [Proposed] A Facebook post or a chat message cannot be marked as replaced or point to what replaced it; the network's notices and answers would. [Proposed] (D-063)

### A network VSU cannot govern

In April 2025, VSU's student media investigated a public Facebook group for VSU people. Begun as a lost-and-found page, it had grown past 12,500 members, accepted members without screening, and allowed anonymous posts. Its creator said he had been removed as moderator under unclear circumstances, and the only visible administrator appeared to be a dummy account. [Established] The investigation reported rage-baiting and slurs, impersonation, and a false and damaging accusation posted with a student's name and photograph. The student's visit to VSU's student services office had mixed results, VSU's ICT staff said tracing anonymous posters would be difficult but not impossible, and the university was reportedly exploring having the page taken down. [Established] (The Amaranth, 2025)

The case shows the limits of a community space VSU does not govern: it belongs to whoever controls it, no one can verify who belongs, no one answerable runs it, anonymity has no limit, and the institution's only lever is a request to the platform. [Proposed] It also shows why the network cannot simply abolish anonymity. Students also post anonymously to raise what they dare not say under their names, and a student criticizing VSU on a network VSU runs needs protection from the institution as well as from other members. [Proposed] (D-046, Q-16) Whether this group is typical is a question for the Facebook study, which names no community group. [Established] (D-044)

### Every batch starts over

Campus knowledge lives wherever it was last said. By the author's observation, answers about scholarships, enrollment, dormitories, and subjects are given in batch group chats, buried under newer messages within days, and given again to the next person who asks; each new batch starts its own chat and asks the same questions; and seniors who answered them year after year eventually stop, out of fatigue rather than rudeness. [Unresolved] This is the heart of the memory hypothesis, and the interviews and the survey can test it. [Proposed]

Organizations face the same loss. VSU's list of accredited organizations for 2022–23 had 57 entries: 35 course-related organizations, 9 religious organizations, 3 interest groups, and 10 student councils. [Established] In January 2025 VSU replaced its colleges with faculties, so several councils on that list now belong to units with new names. [Established] Organizations' records, by the author's observation, live on Facebook pages and officers' personal accounts, and much is lost each time officers graduate. [Unresolved] H3 of the Facebook study tests whether groups drift and are replaced. [Established] (D-042)

### No shared place across roles

VSU staff have an office chat system, Output Messenger, listed for employees only, and VSU's public list of services shows no equivalent for students. [Established] ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md)) VSUEE can carry messages and forums inside courses, where its settings allow. [Established] Outside a course, a student who wants to reach a faculty member they don't have, an adviser, or an office may have nothing but visits, email, the helpdesk, and Facebook. [Unresolved]

### What is not known

The signals above are public records, student journalism, and research from elsewhere. They suggest where to look; they do not show how often VSU students meet these problems or what the problems cost them. [Established] The student survey measures that, the interviews collect specific past incidents, and the Facebook study records what is publicly visible ([Chapter 8](08-evidence.md)). If they find the problems small, the network narrows or stops. [Established]

Three interview probes would test the memory and routing hypotheses directly: when did you last ask something you suspected had been asked before, and where did you look first; have you ever acted on information that turned out to be out of date; and whom would you ask if you wanted to learn something none of your classes teaches? ## Not another social media platform

In one sense the network is social media: people post, reply, join groups, and message each other. In every way that shapes how a platform behaves, such as who pays for it, who sets its rules, what it shows first, what it keeps, and who answers for it, it is designed to differ from the platforms students use now. [Proposed]

| Dimension | Facebook and Messenger, as campus life uses them today | The network, as proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Keep people engaged so that advertising can be sold; ads brought in about 97.6% of Meta's revenue in 2025 [Established] | Help people get campus tasks done, counted in questions answered, answers reused, notices received in time, and exchanges completed [Proposed] (D-061) |
| Who belongs | Anyone; a VSU group can admit strangers without screening [Established] | Verified VSU members, and guests only by invitation [Proposed] |
| Who you are | A personal profile, or an anonymous post where a group allows it [Established] | Your name and VSU role by default, with accountable pseudonyms in chosen sections [Proposed] (D-046, D-065) |
| Who runs a space | Whoever created a group or chat, who may be impossible to identify [Established] | A named owner tied to a real VSU unit, place, recognized organization, or subject [Proposed] (D-062) |
| Who writes the rules | Meta's standards plus each group's own administrators [Established] | Rules written with VSU's community, with notice, reasons, and appeal [Proposed] (D-066) |
| What you see first | A feed ranked to predict engagement [Established] | Unread official notices, then questions in topics you help with and new posts in your communities, in time order, ending when you are caught up, with no hidden scores [Proposed] (D-061) |
| Who you hear from | Whoever is in the group, or online at the moment [Proposed] | Members who chose to help with the topic, and the office responsible [Proposed] (D-071) |
| How long things last | Posts sink in a feed, and chat messages are buried within days [Proposed] | Knowledge kept, dated, and marked when superseded; conversation fades on a published schedule [Proposed] (D-070) |
| Whether something is official | A verified page, whose authority is lost once a post is copied or screenshotted [Proposed] | A label on every post, and a permanent page for each official notice showing whether it is current [Proposed] (D-063) |
| Questions | Answered by whoever replies, with no way to mark the right answer [Proposed] | Answers carry a name and role, and an office can confirm one [Proposed] |
| Recognition | Reactions, follower counts, and shares, public by default [Established] | No public counts or rankings; helpers recognized through confirmed answers and roles their communities give [Proposed] (D-072) |
| Trade | Open to anyone, scammers included [Proposed] | Among verified members, with meeting points on campus [Proposed] |
| Ads, reels, stories, and autoplay | Central to the product [Established] | None [Established] (D-035) |
| Data | Held under Meta's terms, out of VSU's reach [Established] | Held under VSU's privacy program, with VSU's duties under the Data Privacy Act [Proposed] |
| Who it answers to | A private company; VSU can only report content [Established] | VSU and its community; as a state university, VSU must respect students' constitutional rights when it moderates [Proposed] |
| Whether it lasts | Meta closed Facebook Campus in 2022 and Workplace in 2026 when its priorities changed [Established] | Built from open components VSU could host, and continued only if VSU owns it [Proposed] |

Research supports the choice of feed. In a preregistered study of 806 Twitter users, engagement-based ranking amplified emotionally charged, hostile political posts compared with a time-ordered feed, and users did not prefer the posts it chose. [Established] (Milli et al.) In an experiment during the 2020 US election, moving Facebook and Instagram users to a time-ordered feed substantially cut the time they spent there, without significant changes in polarization over three months. [Established] (Guess et al., 2023) For an advertising business, less time is a loss; for a campus utility, it is the point. [Proposed] Regulators are moving the same way: in February 2026, the European Commission preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act for failing to assess and reduce the risks of its addictive design, naming infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications, and its recommender system. [Established]

### Group chats: for talking now, not for knowing later

Much campus coordination runs on Messenger group chats, and 90.6% of Philippine internet users aged 16 and over had used Messenger in the past month. [Established] ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md)) In the author's experience, batches, classes, and organizations each keep their own. [Unresolved] Group chats are good at what they were made for, talking in the moment, and poor at what campus knowledge needs. [Proposed]

| What campus knowledge needs | In a group chat | On the network |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An owner that outlasts any one person | Whoever started the chat, who may graduate, leave, or delete it | A community tied to a real VSU unit or recognized organization (D-062) |
| Answers that can be found later | Buried under newer messages within days | Kept, searchable, and shown before the same question is asked again (D-070, D-071) |
| A way to tell which answer is right | Every reply looks the same | Names, roles, and an office's confirmation (D-063) |
| Knowledge that knows its age | No way to mark an answer outdated, so old screenshots keep circulating | Current, updated, superseded, or archived, with the academic year it applies to (D-063) |
| Help that doesn't depend on who is online | The quality of an answer depends on who happens to see the question | Answers already there before anyone asks, and helpers who chose the topic |
| Helpers who don't burn out | Seniors answer the same questions every year until they stop | An answer given once serves every later batch |

Every row is a proposal. [Proposed] The network would not replace group chats. Coordinating a meeting, a class, or lunch belongs in a chat, and people will keep their chats. The network is where answers, notices, and decisions go to be kept, and it links into chats instead of competing with them: any notice or answer can be shared into a chat as a card that links back to its current version. [Proposed] (D-074)

### What Facebook does better

Facebook will keep doing some things far better than any campus network. It already has everyone, costs nothing to join, connects students with family and friends beyond VSU, and offers mature apps for video and messaging. [Established] The network does not compete for those. A student would keep Facebook for life beyond the campus and use the network for campus tasks, the way staff keep personal apps and use an office chat at work. [Proposed]

### Why not Facebook, done better?

The strongest alternative is to keep campus life on Facebook and run it better: official groups with rules, offices that answer there, and one designated channel for notices. A Philippine college did something like this in June 2026. It named student email and moderated Facebook groups as its official channels and discouraged class group chats, because announcements were drowned out by chatter, rumors ran ahead of official statements, and notifications disturbed rest after hours. [Established] (VMC, 2026)

That would fix some of the problems above at almost no cost. It would not give verified membership, accountable pseudonyms, answers an office confirms and others can find later, knowledge that is marked when it goes out of date, records that outlast officers, data under VSU's care, or rules VSU's community writes. [Proposed] Whether those matter enough to justify a new platform is exactly what a pilot should test, so the pilot compares the network with a well-run Facebook group that has the same office commitment. [Proposed] (D-067) If the Facebook group does as well, the honest result is a policy for official Facebook groups, not a network. [Proposed]

### Facebook Campus, reconsidered for VSU

Meta's Facebook Campus is the closest failed precedent: a college-only section of Facebook, closed in 2022 after Meta concluded that Groups served students best ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md)). [Established] Two of its lessons carry over to VSU. Verified membership did not make students switch, and a campus space needs a reason to be opened every day, which social features alone did not supply. [Proposed]

One lesson reverses. Campus launched in the United States as young people were leaving Facebook: the share of US teenagers who used it fell from 71% in 2014–15 to 32% in 2022. [Established] (Pew Research Center, 2022) At VSU the opposite holds, since almost everyone uses Facebook. [Established] The network therefore faces a stronger incumbent than Facebook Campus did, and it cannot win by being where students are. It can win only on what Facebook and group chats cannot do: routing, memory, and visible authority. [Proposed] What would bring members back daily is still open: notices, schedules if VSU allows, the marketplace, or questions in their subjects. [Unresolved] (Q-46)

### Other kinds of platform

- **Anonymous campus apps** such as Fizz, Yik Yak, and Philippine freedom walls show how much students want to talk about their own campus: by its founders' account, Fizz reached almost every undergraduate at one US college within months of launching. They also show what unlimited anonymity brings, since students there reported bullying and misinformation that were not removed promptly. [Established] ([Chapter 10](10-related-work.md))
- **Peer question-and-answer platforms.** Stack Overflow closes accidental duplicate questions with a link to the original and keeps differently worded ones as signposts, so that people who phrase a problem differently still find the answer. [Established] Brainly, which opened a Philippine site in 2014, runs a points economy for homework help: students earn points by answering and spend them to ask, with rankings and volunteer moderators. [Established] The network borrows the first idea and declines the second, because on a campus network asking must never cost anything. [Proposed] (D-071, D-072)
- **Workplace tools** such as Microsoft Teams, Viva Engage, and Meta's closed Workplace are built for an organization's internal work; the University of Manchester, for one, offers Viva Engage only to staff and postgraduate researchers. [Established] VSU's Microsoft and Google subscriptions include chat and team spaces; whether VSU uses them for student community is not known. [Unresolved]
- **Learning systems** such as VSUEE, Piazza, and Ed Discussion hold discussion inside courses. The network does not; course work stays in VSUEE. [Proposed] (D-064)

## How it would be designed for VSU

[Chapter 3.2](03b-network-design.md) sets out the design in full. Ten principles shape it, each a proposal. [Proposed]

1. **Knowledge moved, not attention held.** No engagement ranking, no hidden scores, no public counts, and a home that ends. Success is counted in questions answered and answers reused. (D-061)
2. **Communities shaped like the university.** Each belongs to a real faculty, department, office, organization, council, dormitory, place, or subject, or to a topic its members start, and has a named owner; inside it are spaces for notices, questions, guides, events, and discussion. (D-062, refining D-034)
3. **Routed to the people who know.** Questions go to members who chose to help with a topic and to the office responsible, and answers that already exist come first. (D-071)
4. **Authority you can see.** Every post carries its information type, only offices post as official, and notices and answers show whether they are current or superseded, and for which academic year. (D-063)
5. **Remember knowledge, let conversation fade.** Answers, guides, and notices stay and keep helping later batches; discussion, listings, and messages fade on a published schedule. (D-070)
6. **Identified by default, safe to speak.** Names and roles by default, and pseudonymous sections where the evidence calls for them, with identities revealed only through a process students share in. (D-046, D-065)
7. **Hand off, don't duplicate.** VSU's systems keep their work, and group chats keep the talk of the moment; the network links into both. (D-064, D-074)
8. **Governed as a commons.** Rules written with the community, protecting speech on student welfare and public matters, with moderation kept apart from institutional response, and with notice, reasons, appeal, and sanctions that rise step by step. (D-066)
9. **Recognition without vanity.** Helping is recognized through confirmed answers and community roles, never through public scores, and knowledge flow is measured for each community, not for each person. (D-072)
10. **Light on data and phones.** An installable web app, text first, with notices and guides readable offline ([Chapter 2](02-intranet.md)).

Behind all ten is one lesson from networks that institutions have run: a network that becomes one more official channel loses its members, so organizations, councils, and interests need as much room as offices ([Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md)). [Proposed]

VSU's own principles point the same way. In January 2025 VSU set out its guiding principles as STRIDES: Service, Truth, Resilience, Integrity, Discipline, Excellence, and Social Justice. [Established] Truth is what the labels and the life of knowledge serve, Integrity what identified participation serves, Service what routing serves, and Social Justice what a free, data-light network serves for students on tight budgets. [Proposed] The same acronym also names VSU's 2026 framework for disasters and emergencies ([Chapter 2.1](02a-emergencies.md)). [Established]

## How it would function

```text
                  VSU's systems of record
     my.VSU · VSUEE · helpdesk · document tracker · WAIS
     website news and announcements · OneVSU (planned)
          ▲ links and hand-offs         │ official feeds, with permission
          │                             ▼
   ┌──────────────────────── the network ─────────────────────────┐
   │ communities shaped like VSU, each with a named owner:        │
   │   notices · questions · guides · events · discussion         │
   │ questions routed to helpers and to the office responsible    │
   │ knowledge labelled, dated, and kept; conversation fades      │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
          ▲ verified members with roles            │ share cards
     students · faculty · staff · offices          ▼
     organizations                             group chats
```

Three scenes show the idea; [Chapter 3.2](03b-network-design.md) has more. [Proposed]

- **A 4:30 a.m. suspension.** A typhoon signal is raised overnight, and under VSU's guidelines a suspension must be announced by 4:30 a.m. [Established] On the network, the authorized office posts it once in the university's official space, labelled official. It reaches every member as an urgent notification, the only kind that passes through quiet hours, and its permanent page shows that it is current. Shared into a group chat, it arrives as a card linking back to that page; a later correction updates the same page instead of starting a new rumor; and a post saying the suspension has been extended carries the unverified label until the office says so. [Proposed]
- **A question every batch asks.** A first-generation student wants to know where a scholarship stipend is claimed. Before her question posts, the network shows the answer a third-year student gave last semester, confirmed by the scholarship office for this academic year, with a note of the one step that changed. She doesn't need to ask, and the student who answered sees, privately, that the answer has now helped someone again. [Proposed]
- **Who knows about this?** A second-year student wants to learn GIS for a thesis idea. She asks in the GIS topic community and chooses answers from students and faculty. Two seniors who chose GIS as a topic they help with reply with the courses they took, and a faculty member who listed GIS among her topics points her to the community where VSU's GIS researchers post their open events. The route follows only what members chose to share. [Proposed]

## How it fits VSU's systems

VSU's systems already hold many of the pieces the network needs: a university Google account for every enrolled student, a learning system with a mobile app, a helpdesk, a document tracker, public feeds of news and announcements, pages listing its graduate faculty's research interests, and an advisory system. [Established] What none of them offers students is a community space that joins these pieces and that VSU and its students govern. [Proposed] Of seventeen concepts the network depends on, the review in [Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md) finds none fully covered for students by VSU's current systems, thirteen covered in part, three not covered at all, and one unknown. [Proposed] The gap is less a missing system than the connections between existing ones, the community space around them, and a memory that keeps what is learned. [Proposed]

Nothing here moves a VSU system or changes its authority. [Established] (D-004) As v0.1 put it, CAMPUS should know enough to connect the university without owning everything the university knows. [Established] (v0.1 §6.14) [Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md) goes system by system.

## Similar systems, and where the network differs

Project rule 5 asks, for every precedent, how CAMPUS differs and which VSU need justifies the difference. [Established] The precedents are reviewed in [Chapter 10](10-related-work.md) and their lessons drawn in [Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md); the pattern is below. [Proposed]

| Kind | Examples | What it shows | How the network differs | VSU need behind the difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial social media | Facebook groups and pages | Campus life runs on general platforms because everyone is already there | Bounded to VSU and governed with it, with no ads or engagement feed | A VSU group that VSU could neither verify nor govern, in 2025 [Established] |
| Group chats | Messenger group chats | Coordination in the moment, and a poor memory | Kept answers and notices that link into chats | Answers lost and asked again each batch, by the author's observation (D-074) |
| Verified campus boards run by companies | Everytime, Dcard, Discord Student Hubs, Facebook Campus | Verified membership plus daily usefulness draws students; verification alone does not | Offices take part, and VSU and its students govern it | Students need offices to answer (Q-17), and a company's board has no place for them |
| Anonymous campus apps | Fizz, Yik Yak, freedom walls | Strong demand for campus talk, and harm when anonymity has no limit | Identified by default, with accountable pseudonyms | Safety to criticize and protection from harm, both at once (Q-16) |
| Peer question-and-answer platforms | Stack Overflow, Brainly | Pointing repeated questions to one answer keeps knowledge in one place; point economies make asking cost something | Routed questions and kept answers, with no points or public scores | First-generation students must be able to ask freely (E-020) |
| Organization and event platforms | CampusGroups, Anthology Engage, Presence | Managing organizations is a mature, paid category | Organizations inside a wider campus network, on open components | 57 accredited organizations whose records turn over with their officers [Established] |
| Institution-run networks | community@brighton and Athabasca's Landing, Mastodon at Innsbruck and SURF, Matrix at TU Dresden and LMU, Iskomunidad at UP Diliman | Universities can run their own; they last when members feel ownership and the institution keeps caring | Students' campus life as the focus, not staff or institutional publishing | VSU has a staff chat and nothing equivalent for students [Established] |
| Learning systems | VSUEE on Moodle, Piazza, Ed Discussion | Course discussion works when teachers take part | Everything outside courses | Course work stays in VSUEE (D-064) |
| Enterprise social networks | Workplace, Viva Engage, Microsoft Teams | Hard to adopt even for Meta, and tied to a vendor's priorities | Built for campus life, on parts VSU could host | Workplace closed in 2026 [Established] |
| University apps and portals | Enderun's app, USTP's app, EVSU's portal, OneVSU (planned) | Institutions build apps for official services and learning | Community and conversation, linking into official apps | What OneVSU will cover for students is unknown (Q-18) |

In one line: campus networks win students with daily usefulness and verified membership, keep them when members feel the space is theirs and their answers keep helping, and fail when anonymity has no limit or their owner stops caring. [Proposed] No system found offers the network's narrower combination: a community space governed with VSU, where offices take part, questions reach the people who know, and every post shows its authority and its age. [Proposed]

## Is it feasible?

[Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md) works through each dimension. The judgments below are the author's, from public sources. [Proposed]

| Dimension | Judgment | Why | What would change it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | High | Every part exists as open-source software that universities already run, from forums to chat servers | Real-time chat at campus scale is the heaviest part (Q-04, D-074) |
| Cost of a pilot | Low | A pilot for a few hundred members fits on one small server costing tens of US dollars a month | Nothing likely |
| Cost at campus scale | Moderate | Servers are modest; people are the main cost, and their time is not yet measured | VSU's decision on ownership and staff (Q-42) |
| Operations | The hardest part | Offices must answer and moderators and helpers must act every week, and no one has committed | An office's commitment (D-068) and a moderation owner (Q-05) |
| Memory | Slow to pay off | A kept answer is worth most in the years after it is given, and one semester shows the least of it | Seeding before launch, and measuring reuse (D-070) |
| Law and policy | Manageable with process, not yet reviewed | The Data Privacy Act, the Safe Spaces Act, and the law against online sexual abuse of children give a host duties | Legal review before real users (Q-36) |
| Adoption | Uncertain | Facebook's network effects are stronger at VSU than they were where Facebook Campus failed, and precedents point both ways | A pilot judged against thresholds fixed in advance (D-067) |
| Institutional fit | Promising, unconfirmed | It carries OVPSAS's services and the Third SOUA's aims, but no one at VSU owns it | OVPSAS's interest; what DIGITS covers (Q-18) |
| One person's capacity | Enough for a prototype, not for a service | No one person can moderate and run a network for 10,000 people | VSU ownership before any growth |

On balance, the network is feasible as a bounded pilot with one committed office, a seeded memory, and a named moderation owner; it is not feasible as a service one person runs, and it is feasible across the campus only if VSU owns it. [Proposed]

## Stages, each with a way to stop

Every stage after the first is a proposal VSU can accept, change, or decline, and each can end in proceed, narrow, redefine, or stop ([Chapter 8](08-evidence.md)). [Proposed]

| Stage | What happens | What it needs | Stop or narrow if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Evidence | The survey, interviews, and Facebook study, with problems judged against the D-038 bars | Permission to run the survey | No network problem clears the bars |
| 1. An office commits | One office agrees to answer student questions within an agreed time for a set period, on a channel it already uses | OVPSAS and one willing office (D-068) | No office will commit (Q-17) |
| 2. Bounded pilot | One community for one semester, its memory seeded before launch, compared with a matched community on a well-run Facebook group with the same office commitment, with thresholds fixed before any data is read | Permission to prototype, a moderation owner, a privacy impact assessment with VSU's data protection officer, volunteers for a guide sprint, and members' consent | The network does no better than the comparison, kept answers are rarely reused, or a safety threshold is crossed (D-067) |
| 3. Wider pilot | More communities, single sign-on with VSU accounts, and official feeds | VSU ICT, the data protection officer, and more offices | Use falls once the novelty fades, or costs outrun the value |
| 4. Institutional decision | VSU decides ownership, hosting, staffing, and any relation to DIGITS | An institutional decision (D-029) | VSU declines, and the network remains a research result |

[Chapter 3.4](03d-network-proposal.md) turns these stages into asks, a one-page pilot proposal, and the questions to expect.

## What would prove or disprove it

- **The problem.** If students already reach offices easily and trust what they find, or the Facebook study's hypotheses count against, the network's case narrows to whatever remains. [Proposed]
- **The offices.** If no office will answer students on any channel, no platform will close the gap, and the pitch's real ask fails before any software matters. [Proposed]
- **The memory.** If members rarely find or reuse kept answers, and keep asking in group chats anyway, the network is a forum with extra steps, and its case narrows to routing and authority. [Proposed]
- **The helpers.** If a few helpers end up answering nearly everything and then leave, routing has failed its own test. [Proposed]
- **The comparison.** If a well-run Facebook group with the same office commitment does as well as the network, the value lies in the commitment, and the result is a policy for official groups. [Proposed]
- **The pseudonyms.** If accountable pseudonyms cannot be made both safe and trusted, the sections that need them stay closed. [Proposed]
- **Ownership.** If no VSU office will own the network after a pilot, it should not grow, however well the pilot goes. [Proposed]

## Hard questions

- **Why would people move?** Everyone is on Facebook, it costs nothing, and it is familiar. The network wins only by doing a few things clearly better, by being useful before it is social, and by starting dense in one community rather than thin across the campus, as Facebook itself did at Harvard in 2004 ([Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md)). [Proposed]
- **Accountability or safety to speak?** The direction is set: participation is identified by default, with anonymous or pseudonymous sections allowed. [Established] (D-046) This chapter proposes how identities would be held and revealed (D-065). Which sections get pseudonyms waits on the Facebook study's H4. [Proposed] (Q-16)
- **Will offices answer?** The gap between students and the university is partly organizational, so office responsiveness, not software, is the real ask of the pitch. [Unresolved] (Q-17) It now comes first, as stage 1. [Proposed] (D-068)
- **Will helpers stay?** Seniors, faculty, and later alumni give the answers, and none of them is paid to. Routing only what each helper chose, capping how many questions reach each person, and letting one answer serve many later askers are the design's answers; whether faculty contributions could count toward their recognized service is a question for VSU. [Unresolved] (Q-47)
- **What should it forget?** A network with a memory must also forget. A first-year student's question about a personal problem should not be findable when she applies for jobs, and the Data Privacy Act allows personal information to be kept only as long as its purpose requires. [Established] The network keeps knowledge and lets conversation fade (D-070); how long each kind lasts, and on what terms members' guides are kept, are open. [Unresolved] (Q-43, Q-44)
- **Who is it for?** VSU's college students, faculty, and staff first; then alumni, and later VSU's applicants and their parents. A knowledge exchange open to every level of education and to the public is a different product, outside CAMPUS's scope. [Proposed] (D-073)
- **Who moderates, and who owns it?** Moderation needs people, rules, appeals, and an owner, and the network as a whole needs an owner and a budget after a pilot. [Unresolved] (Q-05, Q-42)
- **Real-time chat?** Chat is the heaviest part to run, and its cost grows with use ([Chapter 3.2](03b-network-design.md)). The network leaves real-time coordination to group chats in its first version and adds chat only if a pilot shows people want it there. [Proposed] (D-074, Q-04)
- **Minors.** VSU's Integrated High School students hold VSU accounts. [Established] Some incoming college students may also be under 18. [Unresolved] The pilot serves college members only, with stricter defaults for anyone under 18. [Proposed] (D-069)
- **The law.** Education details are sensitive personal information under the Data Privacy Act, a new system must be registered with the National Privacy Commission, and a host takes on duties under the Safe Spaces Act and the law against online sexual abuse of children. [Established] What these require of a VSU-run network needs legal review. [Unresolved] (Q-36)
- **A university moderating speech.** A state university that removes posts from a forum it runs is not a private company doing the same. The Supreme Court has upheld students' freedom of speech on campus, allowing restrictions on conduct that materially disrupts classes, causes substantial disorder, or invades the rights of others. [Established] (Malabanan v. Ramento, 1984) The network's rules start from that standard, and its moderators never remove a post for criticizing VSU. [Proposed] (D-066)
- **One person.** A campus network outlives its builder only if someone keeps caring for it: the University of Brighton's network grew to tens of thousands of users, then declined after years of neglect. [Established] A pilot must end with VSU owning the network or with a clean exit. [Proposed]

## How it grew from v0.1

v0.1 already put a community and knowledge layer (profiles, organization pages, discussions, events) at the foundation of its first build, and it ruled out an unrestricted university social network (v0.1 §7.2). Version 0.2 keeps that limit, since the network is bounded and governed. It adds three things v0.1 did not have: real-time chat, a marketplace, and the explicit aim of replacing Facebook for campus life (D-035). Those three carry most of the moderation and privacy load, so each needs its own evidence. [Established] (D-023) The marketplace has since become its own product, linked from the network ([Chapter 5.2](05b-marketplace.md)). [Established] (D-078)

The October 2026 revision of this chapter builds the rest of the proposal around them: why VSU would need it, what it would do for the university, how it differs from social media, how it would work ([Chapter 3.2](03b-network-design.md)), how it fits VSU's systems and whether it is feasible ([Chapter 3.3](03c-network-fit.md)), and how to propose it ([Chapter 3.4](03d-network-proposal.md)). [Established] The owner's own brainstorming then sharpened its core around routing and memory, kept moderation apart from institutional response, and set limits on recognition and on who the network serves. [Established] These are recorded as proposals D-070 to D-075, with revisions to D-061, D-062, D-063, and D-066, and all await the owner. [Proposed] D-075 also proposes how v0.1's relational model carries into the network, the question D-025 left open. [Proposed]
