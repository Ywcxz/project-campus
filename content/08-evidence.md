## How CAMPUS will find out

The work runs in five streams: problem and user research, institutional systems discovery, technical feasibility, governance and privacy, and research into later directions once they pass a gate. Methods include interviews, observation, surveys, comparative tasks (the current way against a prototype), technical experiments, and Hop-It's Alpha metrics. [Proposed] (v0.1 §11) The first observation study, of public Facebook pages and groups, is set out in [Chapter 8.1](08a-facebook-study.md). [Established] (D-041)

Each product passes these gates in order, and any gate can end in proceed, narrow, redefine, or stop. [Proposed] (adapted from v0.1 §11)

1. **Problem.** Is the problem real and meaningful at VSU?
2. **Value.** Does the product do the job better than what people use now?
3. **Feasibility.** Can it be built and run within acceptable complexity?
4. **Institutional fit.** Can it coexist with VSU's systems and governance?
5. **User value in a pilot.** Do real users keep using it for real tasks?
6. **Expansion.** Is there evidence from users, technology, the institution, and governance all at once?

## Protecting the people who take part

The student survey and the interviews will be the first time CAMPUS handles anyone's personal information, and permission to run the survey is the first thing it asks of VSU (D-083). [Established] Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012, collecting personal information needs the person's consent or another basis the Act allows, and education details such as program and year count as sensitive personal information, with stricter conditions ([Republic Act No. 10173](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2012/ra_10173_2012.html), Sections 3, 12, and 13). [Established] (E-041)

What is settled: the survey runs on a site of its own, its responses go to a private Google Sheet, and interview volunteers sign up on a separate form, so contact details never meet survey answers. [Established] (D-039) The Facebook study records no names, links, quotes, or screenshots, and its workbook stays outside this repository. [Established] (D-041, D-044)

The protocol is published in this chapter before the survey opens: the consent text, how responses are stored, who can see them, when they are deleted, and how the D-038 bars are computed. Responses, contact details, and interview notes never enter this repository. [Established] (D-086)

Three questions must be settled before the survey opens:

- Which parts of the field research need VSU's permission or an ethics review, and from which office, when the researcher is an alumnus working outside VSU? (Q-51)
- Some first-year students may be under 18. Are they screened out, or included with a parent's or guardian's consent? (Q-52)
- What does the consent screen promise, who can see responses and interview notes, and when are they deleted? (Q-53)

## Evidence register

Every claim that matters gets an entry: the claim, the evidence type, the source with its date and a link, the decision it affects, how confidently the source supports it, and when the source was last checked. Evidence that cuts the other way is listed after the table. Observations are recorded apart from interpretations, and evidence must be able to change a decision, not just decorate one. [Established] (v0.1 §18.10)

Confidence follows one rubric. **High:** a primary record, or a peer-reviewed study, that states the entry directly. **Medium:** a reliable secondary report, a preprint, or a primary account no one has checked independently. **Low:** an estimate, an informal account, or a source not yet confirmed to say what the entry claims. **Checked** is the date someone last opened the source and confirmed it still says this; a dash means it has not been rechecked since the entry was written. ## What cuts the other way

Evidence that weakens a hypothesis or a decision is kept beside the evidence that supports it. [Established] (v0.1 §18.10)

- **Wi-Fi coverage (P-19).** By May 2023, close to 98% of the Main Campus had VSU Wi-Fi. [Established] (E-010) Any gap P-19 describes would then be one of capacity or quality, not coverage. [Proposed] Whether students outside the dormitories get usable Wi-Fi during class hours is for the survey and a signal walk to show. [Unresolved] (Q-29)
- **Mobile data (P-15, P-18).** Nationally, Filipino smartphone users spend about 56% of their time on Wi-Fi, and only about 5% rely on mobile data alone. [Established] (E-014) If VSU students are similar, mobile data costs may weigh less in the intranet's case than P-15 and P-18 assume. [Unresolved] (Q-28)
- **Caching (D-056).** The 43% hit rate for a shared university cache was measured in 1999, before most web traffic was encrypted; today about 98% of page loads in Chrome on Windows use HTTPS. [Established] (E-012, E-011) A campus cache can therefore serve only content whose owners take part, which is why D-056 limits caching to VSU's own services, participating sources, and open collections. [Established]
- **A stronger incumbent.** Facebook Campus, a college-only section in 204 schools, closed after Meta concluded that Groups served students best. [Established] (E-006) It launched as US teenagers were leaving Facebook, while at VSU almost everyone uses it, so a VSU network would face a stronger incumbent than Facebook Campus did. [Proposed] (E-035; [Chapter 3](03-social-network.md))
- **Recourse (P-12).** In the one documented case, VSU's ICT staff, legal office, and web team responded, and the group later turned off anonymous posting, though a takedown was still only being explored. [Established] (E-023) Whether students harmed in spaces VSU cannot govern generally find recourse is for the interviews to show. [Unresolved]
- **Seasonal demand (P-14).** Starship estimates its campus delivery robots get about seven months of work a year. [Established] (E-008) Hop-It's pooled demand may fall away during breaks in the same way, which bears on when its Alpha runs. [Unresolved] (Q-19)
