# The Social/Academic Network

*A campus community space that VSU designs and governs*

Much of VSU's campus life already runs on a social network: questions about enrollment and scholarships, notices of suspended classes, organizations recruiting members, students selling books and looking for rooms. That network is mostly Facebook, built to sell advertising and governed from outside the university. [Unresolved] The centerpiece proposal of CAMPUS is VSU's own alternative for campus life: a community space where students, faculty, staff, and offices can do what they now do on Facebook, in a form designed and governed for the university, to connect relevant information to the right people and close the gap between students and the institution. [Established] (D-035) It leads the first pitch, to OVPSAS. Nothing about it has been approved, built, or tested. [Established]

> **In short.** The network is not a better Facebook. It is a place for the jobs campus life needs and Facebook does badly: reaching the right office, getting answers someone stands behind, notices that keep their authority, organizations that keep their records, and trade among people who really belong to VSU. [Proposed] VSU already holds most of the pieces, from a university account for every student to a learning system, a helpdesk, and an advisory system. What it lacks is a community space that joins them and that VSU and its students govern. [Proposed] The software is the easy part. The hard parts are people: offices that answer, moderators who act, rules the community accepts, and an owner inside VSU. [Proposed] Whether students would move is a question only a bounded pilot can answer, and this chapter sets out how to ask it. [Established]

## The idea

### A network that already exists

The case for the network runs in five steps, each a hypothesis to test. [Proposed]

1. VSU's community life already happens on a social network, mostly Facebook and Messenger, which almost every Filipino internet user uses: 94.9% of those aged 16 and over had used Facebook in the past month, according to the Digital 2026 report ([Chapter 9](09-related-work.md)). [Established]
2. That network was built for a different purpose. Advertising brought in about 97.6% of Meta's revenue in 2025, and its feeds are ranked to hold attention. [Established] It knows nothing of VSU's roles, offices, or rules.
3. Many of campus life's problems are problems of routing: a question that should reach an office, a notice that should reach the students it concerns, an answer that should carry the authority of whoever gave it. [Proposed]
4. A network shaped like the university, with verified members, visible roles, channels for real VSU units, and labels on every post, could do those jobs better than a general platform. [Proposed]
5. People will move only for jobs it does clearly better, so the network should aim to win a few specific jobs, not people's attention. [Proposed] (D-061)

Steps 1 and 3 need VSU evidence from the survey, the interviews, and the Facebook study ([Chapter 7.1](07a-facebook-study.md)). [Unresolved] Step 5 is what keeps the network from becoming a smaller copy of Facebook. [Proposed]

### What "social/academic" means

The network is social because it is made of people and groups talking, asking, and trading. It is academic because its shape comes from the university: faculties and departments, offices and their services, organizations and councils, dormitories and places, and the roles of student, faculty member, and staff. [Proposed] It is not a learning system, which VSU already has in VSUEE, and not a portal for official transactions, which DIGITS plans. [Established] It sits between them, as the place where campus life talks and where the university can answer. [Proposed]

The closest idea in the research on shared resources is a commons: a resource a community uses under rules that the community helps make and enforce. Elinor Ostrom's study of commons that lasted for generations found they shared a handful of design principles, from clear boundaries to rules members can change and sanctions that rise step by step. [Established] (Ostrom, 1990) A campus network is that kind of resource, and [Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md) uses the principles to design its governance. [Proposed]

## Why VSU needs it

These are the reasons the author expects field research to confirm, each with the first public signals behind it. None is VSU field evidence yet, and each must clear the D-038 bars before it appears on a pitch slide. [Established] (D-036, D-038)

### The student–institution gap

- **Students who cannot ask at home.** Of the 2,604 first-year students who enrolled at the Main Campus in 2026, 1,248, or 47.9%, are the first in their families to go to college, and 582 were admitted through the affirmative action pathway. [Established] Research elsewhere finds that first-generation students understand the unwritten expectations of college less well than students whose parents went, because they cannot learn them at home. [Established] (Collier and Morgan, 2008) When a parent cannot explain enrollment, scholarships, or which office does what, the answer has to come from peers or from the university, and the network is meant to make both reachable. [Proposed]
- **Offices that are hard to find.** VSU publishes a Citizen's Charter of its services and runs a helpdesk and a document request tracker. [Established] What students lack, by the author's account, is a way to learn which office handles a question before the first visit, and a place where common questions get public answers. [Unresolved] The Third SOUA says student support should also make the campus a place where students feel comfortable asking for help. [Established]
- **The first weeks.** Student Google accounts are issued after the late enrollment period. [Established] The weeks when new students have the most questions are the weeks before they have a VSU account, a gap any design built on those accounts must close. [Proposed] (Q-38)

### Information that loses its authority

- **Many channels and no single trail.** VSU's guidelines announce typhoon suspensions through radio, television, SMS, the website, and other social media, no later than 4:30 a.m. [Established] (BOR Resolution No. 162, s. 2024) WAIS adds localized advisories for VSU account holders, with a mobile app and push notifications planned. [Established] Each channel is reasonable on its own. Together they mean a notice may reach many students as a share or a screenshot, with nothing to show whether it is still current. [Proposed] H1 of the Facebook study tests whether notices change as they travel. [Established] (D-042)
- **The website carries little of daily life.** In early October 2026, the website's announcements feed listed 21 items from July 2021 to September 2026, seven of them from 2026, mostly enrollment and exam schedules. [Established] The day-to-day notices students act on, such as office advisories, scholarship calls, and organization announcements, appear to travel elsewhere, mostly through Facebook. [Unresolved]

### A network VSU cannot govern

In April 2025, VSU's student media investigated a public Facebook group for VSU people. Begun as a lost-and-found page, it had grown past 12,500 members, accepted members without screening, allowed anonymous posts, and was run by administrators who were not clearly identified. [Established] The investigation reported rage-baiting and slurs, impersonation, and a false and damaging accusation posted with a student's name and photograph. The student's visit to VSU's student services office had mixed results, VSU's ICT staff said tracing anonymous posters would be difficult but not impossible, and the university was reportedly exploring having the page taken down. [Established] (The Amaranth, 2025)

The case shows the limits of a community space VSU does not govern: no one can verify who belongs, no one answerable runs it, anonymity has no limit, and the institution's only lever is a request to the platform. [Proposed] It also shows why the network cannot simply abolish anonymity. Students also post anonymously to raise what they dare not say under their names, and a student criticizing VSU on a network VSU runs needs protection from the institution as well as from other members. [Proposed] (D-046, Q-16) Whether this group is typical is a question for the Facebook study, which names no community group. [Established] (D-044)

### Knowledge that leaves with each batch

VSU's list of accredited organizations for 2022–23 had 57 entries: 35 course-related organizations, 9 religious organizations, 3 interest groups, and 10 student councils. [Established] In January 2025 VSU replaced its eight colleges with faculties, so several councils on that list now belong to units with new names. [Established] Organizations' records, by the author's observation, live on Facebook pages and officers' personal accounts, and much is lost each time officers graduate. [Unresolved] H3 of the Facebook study tests whether groups drift and are replaced. [Established] (D-042)

### No shared place across roles

VSU staff have an office chat system, Output Messenger, listed for employees only, and VSU's public list of services shows no equivalent for students. [Established] ([Chapter 9](09-related-work.md)) VSUEE can carry messages and forums inside courses, where its settings allow. [Established] Outside a course, a student who wants to reach a faculty member they don't have, an adviser, or an office may have nothing but visits, email, the helpdesk, and Facebook. [Unresolved]

### What is not known

The signals above are public records, student journalism, and research from elsewhere. They suggest where to look; they do not show how often VSU students meet these problems or what the problems cost them. [Established] The student survey measures that, the interviews collect specific past incidents, and the Facebook study records what is publicly visible ([Chapter 7](07-evidence.md)). If they find the problems small, the network narrows or stops. [Established]

## Not another social media platform

In one sense the network is social media: people post, reply, join groups, and message each other. In every way that shapes how a platform behaves, such as who pays for it, who sets its rules, what it shows first, and who answers for it, it is designed to differ from the platforms students use now. [Proposed]

| Dimension | Facebook, as campus life uses it today | The network, as proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Keep people engaged so that advertising can be sold; ads brought in about 97.6% of Meta's revenue in 2025 [Established] | Help people get campus tasks done, counted in questions answered, notices received in time, and exchanges completed [Proposed] (D-061) |
| Who belongs | Anyone; a VSU group can admit strangers without screening [Established] | Verified VSU members, and guests only by invitation [Proposed] |
| Who you are | A personal profile, or an anonymous post where a group allows it [Established] | Your name and VSU role by default, with accountable pseudonyms in chosen sections [Proposed] (D-046, D-065) |
| Who runs a group | Whoever created it, who may be impossible to identify [Established] | A named owner tied to a real VSU unit, place, or recognized organization [Proposed] (D-062) |
| Who writes the rules | Meta's standards plus each group's own administrators [Established] | Rules written with VSU's community, with notice, reasons, and appeal [Proposed] (D-066) |
| What you see first | A feed ranked to predict engagement [Established] | Unread official notices, then questions you can answer and new posts in your channels, in time order, ending when you are caught up [Proposed] (D-061) |
| Whether something is official | A verified page, whose authority is lost once a post is copied or screenshotted [Proposed] | A label on every post, and a permanent page for each official notice showing whether it is current [Proposed] (D-063) |
| Questions | Answered by whoever replies, with no way to mark the right answer [Proposed] | Answers carry a name and role, and an office can confirm one [Proposed] |
| Trade | Open to anyone, scammers included [Proposed] | Among verified members, with meeting points on campus [Proposed] |
| Ads, reels, stories, and autoplay | Central to the product [Established] | None [Established] (D-035) |
| Data | Held under Meta's terms, out of VSU's reach [Established] | Held under VSU's privacy program, with VSU's duties under the Data Privacy Act [Proposed] |
| Who it answers to | A private company; VSU can only report content [Established] | VSU and its community; as a state university, VSU must respect students' constitutional rights when it moderates [Proposed] |
| Whether it lasts | Meta closed Facebook Campus in 2022 and Workplace in 2026 when its priorities changed [Established] | Built from open components VSU could host, and continued only if VSU owns it [Proposed] |

Research supports the choice of feed. In a preregistered study of 806 Twitter users, engagement-based ranking amplified emotionally charged, hostile political posts compared with a time-ordered feed, and users did not prefer the posts it chose. [Established] (Milli et al.) In an experiment during the 2020 US election, moving Facebook and Instagram users to a time-ordered feed substantially cut the time they spent there, without significant changes in polarization over three months. [Established] (Guess et al., 2023) For an advertising business, less time is a loss; for a campus utility, it is the point. [Proposed] Regulators are moving the same way: in February 2026, the European Commission preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act for failing to assess and reduce the risks of its addictive design, naming infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications, and its recommender system. [Established]

### What Facebook does better

Facebook will keep doing some things far better than any campus network. It already has everyone, costs nothing to join, connects students with family and friends beyond VSU, and offers mature apps for video and messaging. [Established] The network does not compete for those. A student would keep Facebook for life beyond the campus and use the network for campus tasks, the way staff keep personal apps and use an office chat at work. [Proposed]

### Why not Facebook, done better?

The strongest alternative is to keep campus life on Facebook and run it better: official groups with rules, offices that answer there, and one designated channel for notices. A Philippine college did something like this in June 2026. It named student email and moderated Facebook groups as its official channels and discouraged class group chats, because announcements were drowned out by chatter, rumors ran ahead of official statements, and notifications disturbed rest after hours. [Established] (VMC, 2026)

That would fix some of the problems above at almost no cost. It would not give verified membership, accountable pseudonyms, answers an office confirms and others can find later, records that outlast officers, data under VSU's care, or rules VSU's community writes. [Proposed] Whether those matter enough to justify a new platform is exactly what a pilot should test, so the pilot compares the network with a well-run Facebook group that has the same office commitment. [Proposed] (D-067) If the Facebook group does as well, the honest result is a policy for official Facebook groups, not a network. [Proposed]

### Other kinds of platform

- **Anonymous campus apps** such as Fizz, Yik Yak, and Philippine freedom walls show how much students want to talk about their own campus: by its founders' account, Fizz reached almost every undergraduate at one US college within months of launching. They also show what unlimited anonymity brings, since students there reported bullying and misinformation that were not removed promptly. [Established] ([Chapter 9](09-related-work.md))
- **Workplace tools** such as Microsoft Teams, Viva Engage, and Meta's closed Workplace are built for an organization's internal work; the University of Manchester, for one, offers Viva Engage only to staff and postgraduate researchers. [Established] VSU's Microsoft and Google subscriptions include chat and team spaces; whether VSU uses them for student community is not known. [Unresolved]
- **Learning systems** such as VSUEE, Piazza, and Ed Discussion hold discussion inside courses. The network does not; course work stays in VSUEE. [Proposed] (D-064)

## How it would be designed for VSU

[Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md) sets out the design in full. Eight principles shape it, each a proposal. [Proposed]

1. **Tasks, not attention.** No engagement ranking, no public like counts, and a home that ends. Success is measured in tasks done. (D-061)
2. **Shaped like the university.** Channels belong to real faculties, departments, offices, organizations, councils, dormitories, and places, each with a named owner, from a list of units VSU keeps. (D-062, refining D-034)
3. **Authority you can see.** Every post carries its information type, only offices post as official, and labels stay with posts when they are shared. (D-063)
4. **Identified by default, safe to speak.** Names and roles by default, and pseudonymous sections where the evidence calls for them, with identities revealed only through a process students share in. (D-046, D-065)
5. **Hand off, don't duplicate.** Requests, records, course work, and advisories stay in VSU's systems; the network helps people find them. (D-064)
6. **Governed as a commons.** Rules written with the community, protecting speech on student welfare and public matters, with notice, reasons, appeal, and sanctions that rise step by step. (D-066)
7. **Light on data and phones.** An installable web app, text first, with notices and guides readable offline ([Chapter 4](04-intranet.md)).
8. **The community's own.** An institution's network that turns into one more official channel loses its members, so organizations, councils, and interests need as much room as offices ([Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md)).

VSU's own principles point the same way. In January 2025 VSU set out its guiding principles as STRIDES: Service, Truth, Resilience, Integrity, Discipline, Excellence, and Social Justice. [Established] Truth is what the labels serve, Integrity what identified participation serves, Service what reaching offices serves, and Social Justice what a free, data-light network serves for students on tight budgets. [Proposed] The same acronym also names VSU's 2026 framework for disasters and emergencies ([Chapter 4.1](04a-emergencies.md)). [Established]

## How it would function

```text
                  VSU's systems of record
     my.VSU · VSUEE · helpdesk · document tracker · WAIS
     website news and announcements · OneVSU (planned)
          ▲ links and hand-offs         │ official feeds, with permission
          │                             ▼
   ┌────────────────────── the network ──────────────────────┐
   │ channels shaped like VSU: offices · faculties ·         │
   │ organizations · councils · dormitories · places         │
   │ posts with labels: official · community knowledge ·     │
   │ opinion · unverified · scholarly · external             │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
          ▲ verified members with roles
     students · faculty · staff · offices · organizations
```

Two scenes show the idea; [Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md) has more. [Proposed]

- **A 4:30 a.m. suspension.** A typhoon signal is raised overnight, and under VSU's guidelines a suspension must be announced by 4:30 a.m. [Established] On the network, the authorized office posts it once in the university's official channel, labelled official. It reaches every member as an urgent notification, the only kind that passes through quiet hours, and its permanent page shows that it is current. A screenshot shared on Facebook still carries a short link back to that page, a later correction updates the same page instead of starting a new rumor, and a post saying the suspension has been extended carries the unverified label until the office says so. [Proposed]
- **A first-year student's question.** A first-generation student asks in the scholarships channel where a stipend is claimed. A third-year student answers under her name and role, and the scholarship office confirms the answer, which is marked confirmed and dated. The next semester's first-year students find it by searching before they ask. [Proposed]

## How it fits VSU's systems

VSU's systems already hold most of the pieces the network needs: a university Google account for every enrolled student, a learning system with a mobile app, a helpdesk, a document tracker, public feeds of news and announcements, and an advisory system. [Established] What none of them offers students is a community space that joins these pieces and that VSU and its students govern. [Proposed] Of fifteen concepts the network depends on, the review in [Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md) finds none fully covered for students by VSU's current systems, eleven covered in part, three not covered at all, and one unknown. [Proposed] The gap is less a missing system than the connections between existing ones and the community space around them. [Proposed]

Nothing here moves a VSU system or changes its authority. [Established] (D-004) As v0.1 put it, CAMPUS should know enough to connect the university without owning everything the university knows. [Established] (v0.1 §6.14) [Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md) goes system by system.

## Similar systems, and where the network differs

Project rule 5 asks, for every precedent, how CAMPUS differs and which VSU need justifies the difference. [Established] The precedents are reviewed in [Chapter 9](09-related-work.md) and their lessons drawn in [Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md); the pattern is below. [Proposed]

| Kind | Examples | What it shows | How the network differs | VSU need behind the difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial social media | Facebook groups and pages, Messenger | Campus life runs on general platforms because everyone is already there | Bounded to VSU and governed with it, with no ads or engagement feed | A VSU group that VSU could neither verify nor govern, in 2025 [Established] |
| Verified campus boards run by companies | Everytime, Dcard, Discord Student Hubs, Facebook Campus | Verified membership plus daily usefulness draws students; verification alone does not | Offices take part, and VSU and its students govern it | Students need offices to answer (Q-17), and a company's board has no place for them |
| Anonymous campus apps | Fizz, Yik Yak, freedom walls | Strong demand for campus talk, and harm when anonymity has no limit | Identified by default, with accountable pseudonyms | Safety to criticize and protection from harm, both at once (Q-16) |
| Organization and event platforms | CampusGroups, Anthology Engage, Presence | Managing organizations is a mature, paid category | Organizations inside a wider campus network, on open components | 57 accredited organizations whose records turn over with their officers [Established] |
| Institution-run networks | community@brighton and Athabasca's Landing, Mastodon at Innsbruck and SURF, Matrix at TU Dresden and LMU, Iskomunidad at UP Diliman | Universities can run their own; they last when members feel ownership and the institution keeps caring | Students' campus life as the focus, not staff or institutional publishing | VSU has a staff chat and nothing equivalent for students [Established] |
| Learning systems | VSUEE on Moodle, Piazza, Ed Discussion | Course discussion works when teachers take part | Everything outside courses | Course work stays in VSUEE (D-064) |
| Enterprise social networks | Workplace, Viva Engage, Microsoft Teams | Hard to adopt even for Meta, and tied to a vendor's priorities | Built for campus life, on parts VSU could host | Workplace closed in 2026 [Established] |
| University apps and portals | Enderun's app, USTP's app, EVSU's portal, OneVSU (planned) | Institutions build apps for official services and learning | Community and conversation, linking into official apps | What OneVSU will cover for students is unknown (Q-18) |

In one line: campus networks win students with daily usefulness and verified membership, keep them when members feel the space is theirs, and fail when anonymity has no limit or their owner stops caring. [Proposed] No system found offers the network's narrower combination, a community space governed with VSU where offices take part and every post shows its authority. [Proposed]

## Is it feasible?

[Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md) works through each dimension. The judgments below are the author's, from public sources. [Proposed]

| Dimension | Judgment | Why | What would change it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | High | Every part exists as open-source software that universities already run, from forums to chat servers | Real-time chat at campus scale is the heaviest part (Q-04) |
| Cost of a pilot | Low | A pilot for a few hundred members fits on one small server costing tens of US dollars a month | Nothing likely |
| Cost at campus scale | Moderate | Servers are modest; people are the main cost, and their time is not yet measured | VSU's decision on ownership and staff (Q-42) |
| Operations | The hardest part | Offices must answer and moderators must act every week, and no one has committed | An office's commitment (D-068) and a moderation owner (Q-05) |
| Law and policy | Manageable with process, not yet reviewed | The Data Privacy Act, the Safe Spaces Act, and the law against online sexual abuse of children give a host duties | Legal review before real users (Q-36) |
| Adoption | Uncertain | Facebook's network effects, and precedents that point both ways | A pilot judged against thresholds fixed in advance (D-067) |
| Institutional fit | Promising, unconfirmed | It carries OVPSAS's services and the Third SOUA's aims, but no one at VSU owns it | OVPSAS's interest; what DIGITS covers (Q-18) |
| One person's capacity | Enough for a prototype, not for a service | No one person can moderate and run a network for 10,000 people | VSU ownership before any growth |

On balance, the network is feasible as a bounded pilot with one committed office and a named moderation owner, not feasible as a service one person runs, and feasible across the campus only if VSU owns it. [Proposed]

[Chapter 2.4](02d-network-proposal.md) turns these stages into asks, a one-page pilot proposal, and the questions to expect.

## What would prove or disprove it

- **The problem.** If students already reach offices easily and trust what they find, or the Facebook study's hypotheses count against, the network's case narrows to whatever remains. [Proposed]
- **The offices.** If no office will answer students on any channel, no platform will close the gap, and the pitch's real ask fails before any software matters. [Proposed]
- **The comparison.** If a well-run Facebook group with the same office commitment does as well as the network, the value lies in the commitment, and the result is a policy for official groups. [Proposed]
- **The pseudonyms.** If accountable pseudonyms cannot be made both safe and trusted, the sections that need them stay closed. [Proposed]
- **Ownership.** If no VSU office will own the network after a pilot, it should not grow, however well the pilot goes. [Proposed]

## Hard questions

- **Why would people move?** Everyone is on Facebook, it costs nothing, and it is familiar. The network wins only by doing a few things clearly better, by being useful before it is social, and by starting dense in one community rather than thin across the campus, as Facebook itself did at Harvard in 2004 ([Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md)). [Proposed]
- **Accountability or safety to speak?** The direction is set: participation is identified by default, with anonymous or pseudonymous sections allowed. [Established] (D-046) This chapter proposes how identities would be held and revealed (D-065). Which sections get pseudonyms waits on the Facebook study's H4. [Proposed] (Q-16)
- **Will offices answer?** The gap between students and the university is partly organizational, so office responsiveness, not software, is the real ask of the pitch. [Unresolved] (Q-17) It now comes first, as stage 1. [Proposed] (D-068)
- **Who moderates, and who owns it?** Moderation needs people, rules, appeals, and an owner, and the network as a whole needs an owner and a budget after a pilot. [Unresolved] (Q-05, Q-42)
- **Real-time chat or tiered sync?** Chat is the heaviest part to run. Element's sizing guide puts a chat server for up to 500 users, without federation, at one processor core and about 1 GiB of memory plus a database server, but ten cores for 2,501 to 10,000 users. [Established] The pilot can start without chat and add it only if the evidence calls for it. [Proposed] (Q-04)
- **Minors.** VSU's Integrated High School students hold VSU accounts. [Established] Some incoming college students may also be under 18. [Unresolved] The pilot serves college members only, with stricter defaults for anyone under 18. [Proposed] (D-069)
- **The law.** Education details are sensitive personal information under the Data Privacy Act, a new system must be registered with the National Privacy Commission, and a host takes on duties under the Safe Spaces Act and the law against online sexual abuse of children. [Established] What these require of a VSU-run network needs legal review. [Unresolved] (Q-36)
- **A university moderating speech.** A state university that removes posts from a forum it runs is not a private company doing the same. The Supreme Court has upheld students' freedom of speech on campus, allowing restrictions on conduct that materially disrupts classes, causes substantial disorder, or invades the rights of others. [Established] (Malabanan v. Ramento, 1984) The network's rules start from that standard. [Proposed] (D-066)
- **One person.** A campus network outlives its builder only if someone keeps caring for it: the University of Brighton's network grew to tens of thousands of users, then declined after years of neglect. [Established] A pilot must end with VSU owning the network or with a clean exit. [Proposed]

## How it grew from v0.1

v0.1 already put a community and knowledge layer (profiles, organization pages, discussions, events) at the foundation of its first build, and it ruled out an unrestricted university social network (v0.1 §7.2). Version 0.2 keeps that limit, since the network is bounded and governed. It adds three things v0.1 did not have: real-time chat, a marketplace, and the explicit aim of replacing Facebook for campus life (D-035). Those three carry most of the moderation and privacy load, so each needs its own evidence. [Established] (D-023)

The October 2026 revision of this chapter builds the rest of the proposal around them: why VSU would need it, what it would do for the university, how it differs from social media, how it would work ([Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md)), how it fits VSU's systems and whether it is feasible ([Chapter 2.3](02c-network-fit.md)), and how to propose it ([Chapter 2.4](02d-network-proposal.md)). [Established] Its new decisions, D-061 to D-069, await the owner. [Proposed]
