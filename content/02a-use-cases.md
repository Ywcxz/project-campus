# Network Use Cases

*An inventory of campus problems the network could solve, each to test before building*

Chapter 2 names three use cases from the author's experience. This inventory widens the search to 29 candidate problems across six areas of university life, each paired with the use case that would answer it and the platforms that already solve something similar. Every entry is a hypothesis. [Unresolved] The inventory decides what to ask in interviews and surveys; only evidence decides what gets built or goes on a pitch slide. [Established] (D-036)

## How to read it

Each entry states the problem to test at VSU, the use case that would answer it, and platforms to borrow from, as inspiration rather than templates. The stages are proposals. [Proposed]

- **First version:** tests the network's core value directly.
- **Early:** gives people a daily reason to show up, which a new network needs in order to grow.
- **Later:** valuable, but depends on the first two working, or on new rules.
- **Research only:** needs data, consent, or policy that does not exist yet.

A use case enters the first version or the early group only if Facebook clearly serves it badly, it needs VSU people rather than VSU data, and its governance risk is manageable without new institutional policy.

## The student–institution gap

- **Reach the right office.** Getting a question to the office or person who can act on it can take several visits. [Unresolved] The use case: a directory of offices and their services that routes each question to the right desk and shows its status until it is answered. VSU already has an anchor for this. As a government institution it falls under the Ease of Doing Business Act (Republic Act No. 11032), which requires a Citizen's Charter of services, steps, and processing times. [Established] VSU posts a Citizen's Charter 2026 on its website, and it already runs a document request and tracking system and an online helpdesk ([Chapter 9](09-related-work.md)). [Established] The use case may therefore narrow to helping people find the right office or system and handing them into it, while tracking stays with VSU's tools. [Proposed] The same job extends from offices to people: a question routed to members who chose to help with its topic, after any existing answer has been shown ([Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md)). [Proposed] (D-071) *Borrow from:* 311 city-service apps, help desks, Stack Overflow's duplicate links. *Stage:* first version.
- **Answers people can trust.** Students answer each other's institutional questions in unofficial groups, where a wrong answer carries no consequence. [Unresolved] The use case: questions whose answers are marked when an office confirms them, and where every answer carries a name and a role. *Borrow from:* Stack Exchange's accepted answers, Piazza's instructor-endorsed answers. *Stage:* first version.
- **Talk across roles.** Students, faculty, and staff have no easy shared place to reach each other. [Unresolved] The use case: direct and group messages with norms for each role, plus bookable consultation hours. *Borrow from:* Slack, Discord, Calendly. *Stage:* first version.
- **Notices that reach the right people.** Class suspensions, deadlines, and advisories travel as shares and screenshots, and people miss the ones meant for them. [Unresolved] The use case: official channels per office, college, and course, synced first and marked official. VSU already issues campus weather advisories through its Weather Advisory and Information System. [Established] The network would carry such notices to the right channels rather than issue its own. [Proposed] *Borrow from:* emergency alert systems, Telegram channels. *Stage:* first version.
- **Report a campus problem.** A broken light, a water outage, or an unsafe path has no obvious place to go and no feedback. [Unresolved] The use case: reports with a photo and a location, routed to the responsible office, with visible status. *Borrow from:* FixMyStreet, 311 apps. *Stage:* later.
- **Town halls.** Students rarely get to question administrators directly. [Unresolved] The use case: scheduled, moderated question sessions with administrators, where the community votes questions up. *Borrow from:* Reddit's AMAs. *Stage:* later.

## Learning together

- **Student guides.** How-to knowledge about enrollment steps, freshman life, and dorms lives in old posts and in seniors' heads. [Unresolved] The use case: a community wiki where guides are edited, dated, checked against official sources, and marked when superseded (D-063). *Borrow from:* Wikipedia, Notion. *Stage:* early.
- **Course and subject spaces.** Class group chats scatter questions, files, and announcements, then reset every semester. [Unresolved] The use case: a subject community where questions, guides, and pinned materials outlast the semester for later batches. It must not duplicate the learning system VSU already uses: sections, course materials, and grades stay in VSUEE, and ratings of individual teachers are not proposed. *Borrow from:* Discord servers, Ed Discussion. *Stage:* later.
- **Study groups and peer tutoring.** Students who need help and students who can give it rarely find each other outside their own circle. [Unresolved] The use case: open study groups, study rooms, and peer tutors listed by subject. *Borrow from:* Discord study servers, tutoring marketplaces. *Stage:* later.
- **Thesis and research partners.** Finding thesis partners, advisers, and research assistant openings depends on who you know. [Unresolved] The use case: research-interest profiles and open calls from faculty and laboratories. *Borrow from:* ResearchGate, LinkedIn. *Stage:* later, together with research discovery ([Chapter 5](05-later-directions.md)).
- **Topic communities across colleges.** People working on the same problem in different colleges seldom meet. [Unresolved] The use case: open communities by topic, such as a crop, a coastline, or disaster recovery, joining students, researchers, and extension staff. *Borrow from:* subreddits, Stack Exchange sites. *Stage:* later.

## Campus life

- **Organizations.** Recruitment, announcements, and records sit on Facebook pages, and much is lost when officers graduate. [Unresolved] The use case: pages for recognized organizations with members, events, documents, and an officer handover that keeps history. Only organizations VSU recognizes appear. [Proposed] That matters for fraternities, which the Anti-Hazing Act of 2018 (Republic Act No. 11053) regulates. [Established] Religious, sports, and academic organizations get the same treatment as every recognized group. *Borrow from:* CampusGroups, Presence. *Stage:* early.
- **Events and activities.** Events are announced in scattered posts, and students miss what they would have joined. [Unresolved] The use case: one calendar by college, organization, and interest, with reminders. The e-attendance named under DIGITS tracks employees' daily attendance, as the Third SOUA describes it; whether VSU records event attendance anywhere is unknown. [Unresolved] *Borrow from:* Luma, Facebook Events. *Stage:* early.
- **Sports.** Intramurals, tryouts, and court schedules are hard to follow. [Unresolved] The use case: team pages, fixtures, results, and facility schedules. *Borrow from:* league apps. *Stage:* later.
- **Dorms and boarding houses.** Finding a boarding house or a dormmate runs on word of mouth and group posts. [Unresolved] The use case: listings and dorm communities with reviews from residents. The standalone housing site was retired (D-028); this is the same need, inside the network. *Borrow from:* rental listing sites. *Stage:* later.
- **Lost and found.** Getting a lost item back depends on someone seeing the right post. [Unresolved] The use case: a campus board linked to whichever office holds found items. *Stage:* later.
- **Rides home.** Weekend trips home are arranged ad hoc. [Unresolved] The use case: ride and fare-sharing posts between verified members only. *Borrow from:* carpool apps. *Stage:* later.
- **Wellbeing.** Students who are struggling may not know where to turn. [Unresolved] The use case: clear routes to guidance, counseling, and health services. The network points people to those trained to help; it does not host crisis support itself. [Proposed] *Stage:* later, designed with the guidance office.

## Campus economy

- **Marketplace.** Buying and selling books, uniforms, gadgets, and dorm items happens in crowded groups open to strangers and scammers. [Unresolved] The use case: listings between verified VSU members, with categories and agreed meetup points on campus. *Borrow from:* Facebook Marketplace, Carousell. *Stage:* later, once moderation and dispute rules exist.
- **Student services and gigs.** Students who tutor, print, lay out documents, or take photos have no trusted place to offer their services. [Unresolved] The use case: service listings with reviews from verified clients. Errand delivery belongs to Hop-It, and the network links to it. *Borrow from:* Fiverr, Upwork. *Stage:* later.
- **Vendors near campus.** Menus, prices, and opening hours of canteens and market stalls are rarely online. [Unresolved] The use case: vendor pages kept current by the vendors themselves. *Stage:* later, shared with Hop-It.

## Careers, scholarships, and alumni

- **Scholarships.** National programs such as CHED's and DOST-SEI's, local government grants, private sponsors, and university scholarships each announce in different places with different deadlines. [Unresolved] The use case: one searchable list with eligibility, deadlines, reminders, and official verification against scams. *Borrow from:* Handshake, scholarship finders. *Stage:* early.
- **Part-time jobs and assistantships.** Paid work on and near campus is found by luck. [Unresolved] The use case: openings from offices, faculty projects, and nearby employers. *Borrow from:* Handshake, Kalibrr, JobStreet. *Stage:* later.
- **OJT and internships.** Finding a host for on-the-job training is hard, and past interns' experiences go unrecorded. [Unresolved] The use case: host listings with reviews from past interns. *Borrow from:* Glassdoor, Handshake. *Stage:* later.
- **Alumni mentoring.** Alumni who want to help have no channel to reach students. [Unresolved] The use case: mentor profiles, office hours, and alumni question sessions, which also serve the Strategic Plan's alumni-engagement goal. *Borrow from:* ADPList, LinkedIn. *Stage:* later.
- **Where graduates go.** Students and applicants can't see what graduates of a program do next. [Unresolved] The use case: career paths shared by alumni who opt in, beside official tracer data where VSU collects it. *Borrow from:* LinkedIn's alumni pages. *Stage:* research only, because it needs consent and data.

## Voices and stories

- **Student writing and video.** Articles, blogs, vlogs, and guides by students scatter across personal accounts. [Unresolved] The use case: profiles and collections where student work lives and can be found by topic. Student publications keep their editorial independence under the Campus Journalism Act of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7079). [Established] The network would host them, never edit them. [Proposed] *Borrow from:* Medium, Substack, YouTube. *Stage:* later.
- **Student voice.** Students lack a fair way to raise collective concerns and see them answered. [Unresolved] The use case: polls, petitions, and student-government forums, with official responses posted where everyone can see them. *Borrow from:* Reddit polls, civic petition sites. *Stage:* later; it depends on the speech rules (Q-16).
- **A window for aspiring students.** Applicants and parents can't easily see real campus life or ask current students. [Unresolved] The use case: ask-a-student chats with volunteer ambassadors, day-in-the-life posts, and program pages showing where graduates go. *Borrow from:* Unibuddy, campus vlogs. *Stage:* later; it bridges into high-school-to-college bridging ([Chapter 5](05-later-directions.md)).

## What this means for the pitch

The pitch's problem slides use only problems with VSU evidence behind them, and each must clear the bars fixed in D-038 before any data is read. [Established] (D-036, D-038) Three to five such problems, each shown with a quote from an interview, a number from the survey, and a real example, would make the opening. [Proposed] A list of plausible problems without evidence invites the question "how do you know?", and today the honest answer is "not yet." [Established]

The gap problems in the first section are the strongest candidates. They are the most specific to VSU, and most of them touch student services, the remit of OVPSAS, the Office of the Vice President for Student Affairs and Services; reaching faculty also involves academic affairs. [Proposed] Every entry above becomes an interview probe, such as "Tell me about the last time you needed…", and the survey measures how often each problem happens and how much time it costs people. [Proposed] [Chapter 2.4](02d-network-proposal.md) maps these use cases onto the student affairs services OVPSAS already provides, and [Chapter 2.2](02b-network-design.md) shows how the first-version ones would work. [Proposed]
