Open Questions & Decision Register

The control layer for an evidence-driven project

This chapter preserves the uncertainty that remains after the initial conceptual consolidation. It distinguishes questions from decisions and assumptions, identifies evidence gaps, prioritizes unresolved work, records provisional ownership, and defines when decisions must be revisited.

The registers are living project artifacts. Their purpose is to prevent outdated assumptions from silently becoming requirements and to maintain traceability between evidence, decisions, and implementation.

Control Principle

Open questions should become research work, assumptions should remain visible, decisions should record their basis, and meaningful changes should preserve their history.

Status vocabulary

  • Established Supported by evidence, testing, authority, or an explicit decision.
  • Proposed A hypothesis, direction, method, or development proposal under validation.
  • Unresolved A question without sufficient evidence or authority.
  • Investigating Actively researched or tested without a final decision.
  • Deferred Intentionally postponed because it is not required for the current stage.
  • Rejected Intentionally excluded based on evidence, scope, feasibility, governance, or strategy.

Proposed is not established. Deferred is not rejected.

Priority model

  • P0 Blocking or immediate: required for a credible MVP experiment.
  • P1 High: may materially affect architecture, compatibility, or MVP design.
  • P2 Important and parallel: investigate during MVP and validation.
  • P3 Pilot or later: active when institutional operation becomes relevant.
  • P4 Strategic or conditional: keep open until evidence justifies action.

Priority indicates when enough evidence is needed for a responsible decision; it does not mean the question will be answered permanently.

Priority open-question register

The initial P0 register asks whether the problem is meaningful, who the primary users are, which tasks the MVP should test, whether relational discovery adds value, which relationships create that value, what the minimum entity and relationship model is, what data can safely be used, what counts as success or failure, and what would cause the MVP to be revised, narrowed, or stopped.

P1 questions address relationship metadata and time, explicit versus derived relationships, prototype identity, permissions, authority, provenance, existing institutional systems, ownership, interfaces, policies, DIGITS overlap, technical representation, storage, search, deployment, and whether the MVP remains small enough to be a research instrument.

P2 questions address user comprehension, cognitive load, relationship overload, authority distinctions, relationship creation and verification, disputes, history, threat modeling, sensitive combinations, inference, local-first value, resource availability, and a defensible MVP budget.

MVP blocking set

The MVP should initially be controlled by a small set of blocking questions: problem, users, tasks, relational value, valuable relationships, minimum model, safe data, authority, provenance, identity, permissions, threat model, validation criteria, and conditions for revision or stopping.

Production identity, university-wide integration, final enterprise storage, production hosting, institution-wide governance, full local-first operation, future dimensions, and commercialization can remain open while a safely bounded prototype proceeds with synthetic, public, project-created, controlled, or simulated data.

Questions that activate at pilot

When a legitimate pilot pathway emerges, deferred questions become active: what institutional data may be used, who owns it, who sponsors the pilot, what identity system is authorized, what integrations are permitted, what reviews are required, what infrastructure and support are needed, what governance applies, what the pilot budget is, and what evidence determines continuation or termination.

The project should not engineer these requirements in full before a legitimate pilot pathway exists.

Decision register

Current decisions include: CAMPUS is an independently initiated R&D concept; it is a digital-campus architecture rather than isolated features; relationships are first-class; specialized systems retain authority where appropriate; identity, authorization, provenance, privacy, and security are foundational; search and discovery are core MVP capabilities; basic place representation is within the current direction; a full digital twin is outside MVP; future dimensions are conditional; local-first is a research direction; AI is not foundational; prototype data must be safely authorized; the prototype is both software and research instrument; strategic alignment is not endorsement; the CAMPUS/DIGITS relationship is unresolved; production architecture remains open; and expansion requires evidence.

Each decision should record its status, evidence or rationale, and trigger for revisiting it.

Assumption register

Working assumptions include that university resources can be meaningfully represented as relationships, users may benefit from relational discovery, context may reduce effort, relationships can coexist with institutional records, provenance can be understood, permissions can be implemented without disproportionate complexity, temporal relationships may matter, a bounded model can support useful scenarios, local-first may provide value in some contexts, and CAMPUS can remain a layer rather than a competing system of record.

Assumptions should be validated, revised, retired, rejected, or promoted to established decisions only when appropriate evidence exists.

Evidence register

For each significant claim or decision, record the claim or finding, evidence type, source, date, confidence, interpretation, affected decision, contradictory evidence, relevance, and responsible researcher. Evidence classes include institutional documents, interviews, observations, surveys, technical experiments, usability tests, benchmarks, security and privacy analysis, scholarly literature, system documentation, stakeholder consultation, and prototype evidence.

Observation must remain distinct from interpretation. Evidence should be capable of changing a preference rather than serving only to justify an existing one.

Decision-making rules

  • 01 Evidence before expansion.
  • 02 Problem before feature.
  • 03 Relationship before accumulation.
  • 04 Integration before duplication.
  • 05 Permission before institutional data.
  • 06 Provenance before trust claims.
  • 07 Prototype before production commitment.
  • 08 Reversibility where possible.
  • 09 Negative evidence is valid evidence.
  • 10 Institutional decisions remain institutional.

Decision gates

Gate 1 asks whether the problem is meaningful. Gate 2 asks whether relationships add value beyond appropriate existing methods. Gate 3 asks whether the architecture is technically feasible. Gate 4 asks whether CAMPUS can coexist with institutional systems and governance. Gate 5 asks whether the tested capability provides sufficient user value. Gate 6 asks whether evidence justifies another dimension.

Outcomes at each gate may include proceeding, revising, narrowing, remaining research-only, deferring, rejecting, or stopping.

Immediate next actions

  1. 01 Freeze the conceptual baseline and record new ideas as proposed changes.
  2. 02 Resolve the P0 question set.
  3. 03 Conduct institutional discovery in parallel.
  4. 04 Specify experiments for the highest-risk assumptions.
  5. 05 Build the smallest functional vertical slice.
  6. 06 Record evidence continuously.
  7. 07 Conduct the first validation gate before expansion.

Blueprint change control

Each meaningful revision should record version, date, change, reason, evidence, affected sections, decision, unresolved consequences, and owner. Changes may be clarifications, evidence updates, architectural changes, scope changes, research findings, institutional discoveries, or decision reversals.

Previous decisions should be preserved rather than silently overwritten. This keeps the Blueprint a living research document with a traceable history.

Blueprint v0.1 Baseline

CAMPUS is an independently initiated R&D concept for a persistent, permission-aware, provenance-aware relational layer. It prioritizes infrastructure, relationships, and evidence over feature count, premature scale, and unvalidated complexity.

Maturity statement

Blueprint v0.1 is mature enough to support technical experiments, bounded prototype development, user research, institutional discovery, architecture evaluation, research proposals, early technical discussions, and controlled presentations.

It is not sufficient to justify institution-wide deployment, production integration, unrestricted institutional data, final technology selection, full-scale implementation, adoption claims, or commercialization claims.

Final decision principle

The project should be allowed to become smaller, different, or even unnecessary if the evidence indicates that this is correct.

The purpose of the Blueprint is not to prove that the entire CAMPUS vision should exist. It is to create a disciplined framework for discovering what should exist, what should not, what should be integrated, what should remain separate, what should be tested, and what evidence is required before each decision.

Control Layer Conclusion

The complete control structure is: Open Questions → Priorities → Owners → Evidence → Assumptions → Decisions → Gates → Actions → Change Control.

The next phase is evidence generation: Problem → Evidence → Prototype → Test → Decision.
