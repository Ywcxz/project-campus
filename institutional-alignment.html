VSU / DIGITS Institutional Alignment

Strategic context is not institutional endorsement

This chapter establishes how CAMPUS relates to the strategic and digital-transformation context of Visayas State University. It does not claim that VSU has requested, endorsed, adopted, funded, or approved CAMPUS.

It identifies where the research direction may be relevant to existing institutional priorities and defines what must be investigated before proposing an institutional relationship, integration, pilot, or adoption. Alignment is therefore a research and validation question, not evidence of institutional commitment.

Alignment Boundary

The VSU Strategic Plan and DIGITS provide context for evaluating CAMPUS. They are not specifications, mandates, proof of inadequate systems, or commitments to adopt the proposed architecture.

VSU strategic context

The VSU Strategic Plan 2017-2027 is used as a reference framework for identifying relevant problems, opportunities, and institutional directions. Relevant goals include World-Class Education, Globally Competitive Science and Technology, Empowered Communities, Sustainable Resource Generation, Client-Centered Governance, Versatile Spaces for Innovation, and Strong Alumni Engagement.

These goals are not evidence that CAMPUS is the intended mechanism for achieving them. They are reference points against which proposed capabilities can be examined through institutional consultation, systems discovery, user research, and prototype testing.

Potential alignment

Potential relevance includes relationship-oriented discovery of programs, courses, people, learning resources, and opportunities; researcher and expertise discovery; community knowledge and organizations; services and institutional resources; place and facility relationships; governance-oriented information access; and persistent alumni and pathway relationships.

These are proposed contributions, not demonstrated strategic impact. The strongest possible alignment is architectural: the strategic goals describe a university coordinating people, education, research, communities, resources, services, physical spaces, innovation, alumni, and governance. CAMPUS investigates whether a relational foundation could help connect those activities without creating a separate system for every goal.

DIGITS context

VSU's publicly described DIGITS roadmap is relevant institutional context for digital governance, learning, research and innovation, stakeholder experience, smart and green campus development, infrastructure, cybersecurity, digital culture, sustainability, analytics, automation, and future AI capabilities.

Public descriptions may mention initiatives such as OneVSU Mobile, OneVSU Portal, OneVSU ERP, an executive dashboard, electronic attendance tracking, and MATS. Their internal architecture, implementation status, ownership, data model, interfaces, deployment model, and governance arrangements must not be assumed without institutional discovery.

Possible relationships with DIGITS

CAMPUS should not assume that it is part of DIGITS, separate from it, superior to it, a replacement for it, already incorporated into it, technically compatible with it, or institutionally authorized by it.

  • A Complementary R&D layer.
  • B Experimental or test environment.
  • C Component of a broader university ecosystem.
  • D Independently developed system with approved integrations.
  • E Alternative or superseded direction.
  • F Research-only outcome.

All six possibilities remain open until sufficient institutional evidence is available.

Institutional systems discovery

Before proposing integration, the project should understand current and planned systems, their functions, authorities, owners, operators, interfaces, identity mechanisms, data policies, security controls, infrastructure, roadmaps, and operational capacity.

The investigation should answer which systems are authoritative for particular information, what may be replicated or only referenced, who can approve access, what privacy and security requirements apply, how roles and permissions work, what hosting is available, and where architectural gaps or overlaps actually exist.

Discovery Purpose

The purpose is not to find reasons to justify CAMPUS. It is to determine where CAMPUS is useful, unnecessary, complementary, constrained, or inappropriate.

Institutional architecture boundary

A central question is what CAMPUS should own and what it should only reference, discover, index, or connect to. Person identity may be an integration concern; enrollment may remain a reference and discovery concern; course and publication data may be cached or linked; facility information may be represented; community discussion may be CAMPUS-native; and opportunities may be represented or integrated according to their authority.

This boundary prevents the relational layer from becoming an accidental second system of record.

Institutional validation maturity

Institutional engagement should be described accurately. The project distinguishes conceptual recognition, technical interest, institutional alignment, permission to prototype, pilot participation, formal collaboration, adoption, and commercialization or external deployment.

Interest is not permission. Permission is not a pilot. A pilot is not adoption.

Stakeholder discovery

Institutional discovery should include leadership, ICT and technical personnel, academic units, research units, administrative and service units, students, alumni, external stakeholders, and other relevant communities. Each sees different systems, workflows, authorities, constraints, and forms of value.

The preferred sequence is strategic context, system discovery, stakeholder research, architecture mapping, bounded prototype, technical validation, user validation, and institutional evaluation. This is more reliable than moving directly from concept to presentation to adoption proposal.

Institutional fit criteria

Before proposing a pilot, CAMPUS should evaluate strategic fit, user need, technical fit, data-governance fit, security, privacy, operational capacity, governance, economic cost, and research value. Technical possibility alone is not sufficient reason for deployment.

Current alignment position

It is established that the VSU Strategic Plan provides relevant strategic context, publicly described digital-transformation activity exists under DIGITS, and existing or planned systems must be understood before CAMPUS can responsibly claim a specific role.

It remains proposed that CAMPUS could provide a relationship-oriented layer, complement broader initiatives, or serve as an R&D environment for discovery, interoperability, provenance, identity, and local-first experiments. It remains unresolved whether CAMPUS overlaps with existing systems, which integrations are possible, whether a pilot would be permitted, and whether the project should remain independent, become complementary, or lead to another outcome.

Institutional Question

Given VSU's strategic priorities, existing systems, institutional constraints, and actual user needs, where, if anywhere, could a relationship-oriented digital-campus architecture create meaningful and demonstrable value?
