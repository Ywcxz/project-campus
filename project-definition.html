Project Definition

The object CAMPUS is trying to investigate

CAMPUS is an independently initiated research and development concept for an experimental digital-campus architecture for universities. It investigates whether a persistent, relationship-oriented digital layer can improve how people discover, connect, interact with, and maintain continuity across the university's people, organizations, education, knowledge, research, services, places, opportunities, and resources.

CAMPUS is therefore defined primarily as an architectural and relational model, rather than as a collection of independent software features. Its immediate objective is to investigate and demonstrate this model through a bounded functional prototype and supporting research, not to build a complete digital replacement for the university.

Central Hypothesis · Proposed

University information, people, services, knowledge, opportunities, and resources may already exist across different institutional and community contexts, but the relationships among them are often difficult to discover, navigate, maintain, and use. A persistent relationship-oriented digital layer may improve discovery, interaction, continuity, and connection across these resources.

What “digital campus” means here

A digital campus is not simply a collection of online services or a university website. CAMPUS explores a digital representation of the relationships that make the university function as a connected environment: people, programs, courses, organizations, facilities, research, knowledge, events, services, opportunities, and external partners.

The proposed digital layer must preserve distinctions of authority, provenance, permissions, privacy, and context. It is an interaction and relationship layer over the university, not a virtual replica intended to replace physical campus life or existing systems.

The relational principle

Prioritize relationships and infrastructure over isolated features.

Instead of treating university information as disconnected pages, records, applications, or directories, CAMPUS proposes representing relevant entities and the relationships among them. A student might be related to a program, course, instructor, organization, event, research project, publication, facility, opportunity, and community.

The exact relationship model remains subject to technical research and validation. The categories described here are conceptual entities, not a finalized database schema.

A layer, not a replacement

Existing systems may remain authoritative for enrollment, student records, finance, human resources, learning management, research repositories, library services, identity management, and institutional reporting.

CAMPUS investigates how a relational layer could connect discovery and interaction across those systems and resources, subject to interfaces, permissions, data governance, security requirements, and technical feasibility. Whether it should link, synchronize, query, index, or otherwise interact with a particular system remains unresolved until the relevant infrastructure is investigated.

Provenance and identity

CAMPUS must distinguish official institutional information, community-generated knowledge, personal perspectives, unverified information, and research or scholarly knowledge. Increased connectivity without context could also increase the spread of incorrect, outdated, misleading, or improperly attributed information.

Identity, authorization, permissions, and relationship continuity are foundational concerns. CAMPUS does not currently assume that it should create an independent institutional identity system; integrating with existing identity infrastructure may be preferable, subject to technical and institutional constraints.

Architectural directions

The broader architecture may eventually include community and knowledge, curriculum continuity and learning records, education to career pathways, research discovery, spatial campus capabilities, local-first infrastructure, GIS, analytics, and AI. These are potential research domains, not automatic MVP requirements.

A local-first or hybrid architecture is likewise a proposed research direction. It could support resilience and continuity through university-controlled infrastructure while synchronizing appropriate information when connectivity is available. Synchronization, conflict resolution, security, and operating scope remain unresolved.

Boundaries

CAMPUS is not currently defined as a replacement for the university ERP, LMS, student information system, HR or finance systems, research repositories, website, directory, GIS, digital twin, AI chatbot, or metaverse. It is also not an assumption that all university data should be centralized or publicly accessible.

R&D Object · Proposed

Can a university benefit from a persistent, permission-aware, provenance-aware, relationship-oriented digital layer that connects people, organizations, knowledge, education, research, places, services, opportunities, and institutional resources while interoperating with existing specialized systems?

Chapter Boundary

The prototype exists to make this proposition testable. The research program must determine whether it is useful, technically feasible, institutionally appropriate, governable, secure, and valuable enough to justify further development.
